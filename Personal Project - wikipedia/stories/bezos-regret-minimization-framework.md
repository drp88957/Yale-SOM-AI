# Bezos's Regret-Minimization Framework: Leaving a Great Job to Sell Books Online

**One-line hook:** Bezos decided to quit a top Wall Street job by imagining himself at 80 looking back.

## The Story
In 1994 Jeff Bezos was a rising star at the hedge fund **D. E. Shaw** in New York. While studying the fast growth of the internet, he and the firm explored the idea of an "everything store" online. Bezos concluded that **books** were the best place to start, because there were far too many titles for any physical store to stock.

His boss, **David Shaw**, took him on a long walk in Central Park and pointed out that the idea might make more sense for someone who didn't already have a good job. Bezos struggled with the choice. He framed it with what he called a **"regret-minimization framework"**: he imagined himself at **age 80** and asked which choice he'd regret. He realized he would not regret trying and failing, but he might always regret **never trying** to be part of the internet.

He quit, and he and his wife MacKenzie moved to Seattle. MacKenzie drove part of the way while Jeff typed up revenue projections on his laptop. The company became Amazon.

## The Lesson
**For big, uncertain life choices, ask which one your older self would regret more.** People tend to regret the chances they didn't take more than the attempts that failed.

## When to Use This
- You're weighing a safe job against a risky opportunity
- Fear of failure is making a choice for you
- You keep putting off a project "until the timing is right"
- You're advising someone on a career move

## Try This
- Write two short letters from your 80-year-old self: one after you took the leap, one after you didn't. Notice which one hurts.
- Separate the real downside (what you'd actually lose) from the fear of looking foolish.
