# How Kobayashi Doubled the Hot-Dog Record

**One-line hook:** A slim young man from Japan doubled the world hot-dog-eating record by asking a better question than everyone else.

## The Story
At the Nathan's Famous hot-dog contest on Coney Island, the record stood at about **25 hot dogs in 12 minutes**. In 2001, a newcomer named **Takeru Kobayashi**, much smaller than many rivals, ate **50**. He nearly doubled the record on his first try.

His secret was how he **framed the problem**. Other competitors asked, "How do I eat more hot dogs?" Kobayashi asked, "How do I make hot dogs **easier to eat**?" He experimented. He broke each hot dog in half. He **separated the sausage from the bun**, ate the sausages quickly, then dunked the buns in water so they went down faster. He practiced, recorded his training and adjusted his body movements.

Levitt and Dubner also note that he ignored the **"limit"** everyone believed in. Others treated 25 or so as close to the human maximum. Kobayashi didn't accept that barrier.

## The Lesson
How you **define the problem** decides which solutions you can see. Redefine it, and question the limits everyone else takes for granted.

## When to Use This
- You're competing in a field where everyone uses the same approach
- Progress has stalled at what seems like a natural ceiling
- You're tackling a big goal and need a new angle

## Try This
- Rewrite your problem statement three different ways and see which opens new solutions.
- Break the task into parts and ask which one is the real bottleneck.
