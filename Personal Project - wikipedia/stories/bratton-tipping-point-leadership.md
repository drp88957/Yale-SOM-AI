# Bill Bratton Made His Officers Ride the Subway

**One-line hook:** To convince police bosses the subway was dangerous, Bratton skipped the reports and made them experience it.

## The Story
**Bill Bratton** turned around policing in New York in the early 1990s without a big budget increase. The authors use his story to explain **tipping point leadership**: how to change an organization quickly with limited resources by focusing on a few points of high influence.

The first hurdle was **cognitive**: people did not believe there was a problem. When Bratton ran the New York Transit Police, senior officials didn't see the subway as dangerous, because they drove to work. So Bratton made them **ride the subway themselves**, including at night. Seeing the fear, the crime, and the disorder firsthand changed their minds faster than statistics ever could.

He also dealt with **limited resources** by focusing them on the few places and crimes that mattered most, and with **motivation** by putting key leaders under the spotlight in regular meetings where precinct commanders had to explain their results in front of peers. Change spread from those key people to the rest of the force.

## The Lesson
You don't need to change everyone at once. **Make people see the problem firsthand, focus on the few hot spots, and win over the key influencers**, and the rest will follow.

## When to Use This
- Your team doesn't believe a problem is serious
- You're leading change with no extra budget
- A slide deck of data isn't moving decision-makers
- You need a large organization to shift behavior quickly

## Try This
- Instead of presenting data, take a decision-maker to see the problem: a customer call, a factory floor, a support queue.
- Identify the two or three people whose behavior others copy, and focus your effort on them first.
