# The Venture Capitalist Who Handed Founders His Critics

**One-line hook:** A VC gave entrepreneurs a list of every founder he'd worked with, including those who might complain, and lost a deal because of his generosity.

## The Story
David Hornik, a venture capitalist, behaves very differently from many in his field. He gives entrepreneurs fast answers, helps founders he won't fund, and gives them a **full list of every entrepreneur he has worked with** so they can check his references without him picking only the happy ones.

Grant tells of a founder, Danny Shader, whom Hornik badly wanted to fund. Hornik made an offer and, as usual, encouraged Shader to talk to other people and take his time. Shader did, and **chose a different investor**, one who had pushed him harder. Hornik's giving seemed to have cost him. But Grant follows the longer arc: the relationship with Shader didn't end there, and over time many founders choose Hornik precisely because of the trust his reputation builds. Grant uses Hornik to show that givers can be both at the **bottom and the top** of success; what matters is how they give.

## The Lesson
**Giving can cost you in the short run but builds a reputation that pays off over time.** Generosity works best when combined with judgment about whom you're dealing with.

## When to Use This
- You're tempted to pressure someone to close a deal quickly
- You're deciding how transparent to be with a client or partner
- You lost an opportunity after being fair and wonder if it was worth it
- You're building a reputation in a small industry

## Try This
- Offer references or information the other side would normally have to dig for.
- After losing a deal, stay helpful to that person; keep the door open.
