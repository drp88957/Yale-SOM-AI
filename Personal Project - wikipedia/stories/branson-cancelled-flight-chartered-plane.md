# The Cancelled Flight That Gave Branson the Idea for an Airline

**One-line hook:** Stranded by a cancelled flight, Branson chartered a plane, sold seats to the other stranded passengers, and found a business.

## The Story
Richard Branson tells of being stuck in the Caribbean when his flight to the **British Virgin Islands was cancelled**. The other passengers grumbled and waited. Branson did something different. He called around and found he could **charter a plane**. He divided the cost by the number of seats, borrowed a **blackboard**, wrote "Virgin Airways" with a one-way price on it, and walked around the airport selling seats to the other stranded travelers.

The plane filled, everyone got where they were going, and Branson covered his cost. More importantly, he saw that airline customers were badly served and that there was room for someone who would treat them better. That experience fed into his later decision to start **Virgin Atlantic**, despite having no airline experience.

This fits the spirit of the book's title: when something seems worth doing, **stop over-thinking and just do it**, and learn along the way.

## The Lesson
**A frustration you share with many others is often a business opportunity.** Act quickly, with a small test, rather than waiting for a perfect plan.

## When to Use This
- Something goes wrong and everyone is just complaining
- You notice customers in an industry being treated badly
- You have an idea but no experience in that field
- You're overplanning and need a nudge to act

## Try This
- Next time you're frustrated by a service, write down how you'd fix it and who'd pay for that fix.
- Run a "blackboard test": offer your idea to real people today, in the simplest possible form.
