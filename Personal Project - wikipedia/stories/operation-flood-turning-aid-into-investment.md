# Operation Flood: Using Foreign Surplus to Build Indian Dairies

**One-line hook:** Free food aid could have crushed India's dairy farmers, so Kurien sold it to fund their growth.

## The Story
European countries had large **surpluses** of dairy products such as milk powder and butter oil. Giving it to India as aid sounded generous. But Kurien saw the danger: cheap foreign milk flooding Indian cities would **undercut local farmers** and kill the dairy industry before it grew.

His answer was **Operation Flood**. The donated products were not given away. They were recombined into milk and **sold** in Indian cities at fair prices, so local farmers were not undercut. The money raised was used to build **dairy cooperatives, processing plants and supply links** that connected village milk producers to big city markets.

Over time, the project helped make India one of the world's largest milk producers. Kurien turned a gift that could have caused **dependence** into **investment** in farmers' own capacity.

## The Lesson
Help that ignores incentives can hurt the people it means to help. **Redesign the flow of resources** so it builds local strength instead of replacing it.

## When to Use This
- Receiving a free resource that might make your team dependent
- Designing aid, subsidies or discounts
- A big player offers help that could kill smaller players
- Thinking through the second-order effects of a "generous" policy

## Try This
- Before accepting help, ask: "What does this do to local incentives in two years?"
- Look for ways to turn one-time help into lasting capacity (training, tools, infrastructure).
