# Van Halen's "No Brown M&M's" Rule

**One-line hook:** A rock band's famously diva-like demand was really a clever safety test.

## The Story
Van Halen's touring contract included a now-famous line: a bowl of **M&M's backstage, with all the brown ones removed**. It looked like pure rock-star excess.

Singer **David Lee Roth** later explained the real reason. Van Halen's stage show was huge, with heavy equipment and complicated electrical and structural requirements spelled out in a thick contract rider. If a local promoter skipped details, the stage could collapse or someone could get hurt. Roth couldn't check every bolt himself. So the M&M's clause was buried in the middle of the technical requirements. If he walked backstage and saw **brown M&M's**, he knew the promoter **hadn't read the contract carefully**, and the band would recheck everything.

Levitt and Dubner pair this with the biblical story of **King Solomon**, who proposed cutting a disputed baby in half to reveal which woman was its real mother. Both are examples of getting people to **reveal the truth through their own actions** when you can't easily see it.

## The Lesson
When you can't watch everything, design a **small, cheap test** that reveals whether people are paying attention, or who they really are.

## When to Use This
- You're relying on vendors, contractors or new hires to follow detailed instructions
- You need to know quickly whether someone actually read the brief
- You're trying to separate serious candidates or partners from careless ones

## Try This
- Add a small, specific, easy-to-check instruction to an important brief or application.
- When the test fails, treat it as a signal to inspect the bigger things.
