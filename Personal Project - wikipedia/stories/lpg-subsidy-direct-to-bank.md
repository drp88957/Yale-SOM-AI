# Plugging the LPG Subsidy Leak

**One-line hook:** When a subsidy goes into the price, the cheats get it too; when it goes into a verified bank account, it reaches the right person.

## The Story
India subsidized **cooking gas (LPG)** by selling cylinders below cost. That created a big gap between the subsidized price and the market price, and the gap invited **leakage**. Fake or duplicate connections were used to get cheap cylinders, and subsidized gas was diverted to commercial use, such as restaurants.

The authors describe how linking the subsidy to **Aadhaar and bank accounts** changed the incentives. Customers paid the market price for cylinders, and the subsidy was sent **directly to their bank account**. Because Aadhaar removed duplicates, each real household could get its benefit, while fake and duplicate connections lost their value.

This became a key example of **Direct Benefit Transfer**. The broader point in the book is that many welfare problems are really **identity and payment problems**: if you can't tell who is who, money leaks.

## The Lesson
If a benefit leaks, **look at where the price gap sits**. Moving the subsidy from the product to the verified person removes the reason to cheat.

## When to Use This
- A discount, perk or budget keeps being misused
- Designing incentives that people will try to game
- Fixing a process where you can't tell who really qualifies
- Choosing between subsidizing a product or a person

## Try This
- Map where value "leaks" in your process. Ask: where is there a gap someone can profit from?
- Tie rewards to verified identity or outcomes, not to easy-to-copy proxies.
