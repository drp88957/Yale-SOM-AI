# India Stack: Building Public Rails, Not Just Apps

**One-line hook:** Instead of building one big government app, India built shared layers that anyone could build on.

## The Story
Once Aadhaar gave people a digital identity, Nilekani and Shah describe a larger idea: **India Stack**, a set of open digital building blocks. The layers remove friction step by step. **Presence-less**: you can prove your identity remotely using Aadhaar authentication and e-KYC. **Paperless**: documents can be signed and stored digitally (eSign, digital lockers). **Cashless**: money can move easily between accounts through systems such as the **Unified Payments Interface (UPI)**.

The key design choice was to make these **public, open platforms**. The government provided the rails, and banks, startups and others built products on top. That meant competition happened on services for users, while the shared infrastructure stayed neutral.

The authors argue that this approach lets a developing country **leapfrog** older systems. Instead of each company building its own identity check or payment network, everyone uses common rails, lowering costs and opening the market to new players.

## The Lesson
Sometimes the biggest impact comes from **building the shared foundation** rather than the final product. Good rails let many others build things you never imagined.

## When to Use This
- Several teams keep rebuilding the same basic tool
- Deciding whether to build a product or a platform
- Thinking about how to encourage competition in a market
- Exploring how technology can include people left out

## Try This
- Find one thing your teams each rebuild separately. Could it become a shared "rail"?
- Ask: "If we opened this to others, what might they build?"
