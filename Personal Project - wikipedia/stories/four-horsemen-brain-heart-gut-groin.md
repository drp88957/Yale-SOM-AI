# Brain, Heart, Gut, Groin: How the Four Giants Hook Us

**One-line hook:** Galloway explains the power of Amazon, Apple, Facebook, and Google by the part of our body each one appeals to.

## The Story
Galloway argues that each of the "Four Horsemen" became so powerful by appealing to a deep, ancient human instinct, which he maps to a part of the body:

- **Google = the brain.** It answers our questions and has become the place we turn to for knowledge, almost like an all-knowing authority.
- **Facebook = the heart.** It taps our need for love, connection, and belonging, keeping us tied to friends and family.
- **Amazon = the gut.** It feeds our instinct to gather and consume, more stuff, faster, cheaper, a modern version of our hunter-gatherer drive.
- **Apple = the groin.** It appeals to our desire to look attractive and signal status, which is why its products work like luxury goods.

The point is that these companies don't just offer useful products. They **plug into instincts** that evolved long before technology existed, which makes them extremely hard to compete with or resist. Galloway uses this to explain both their huge profits and why we hand them so much time, data, and trust.

## The Lesson
The strongest products connect with **basic human instincts**, not just rational needs. Ask which instinct a product serves, whether you're building one or trying to resist one.

## When to Use This
- You're designing a product or pitch and wondering why it isn't sticking
- You're thinking about the power of Big Tech in society
- You want to understand why you spend so much time on a platform
- You're positioning your business against a giant

## Try This
- For your product, write down which instinct it serves: knowledge, love, consumption, or status. Strengthen that.
- Notice which of the four you used most today, and which instinct pulled you there.
