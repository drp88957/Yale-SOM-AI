# The Biggest Leak of the Decade Almost Died Over an Email Setting

**One-line hook:** Edward Snowden reached out to Glenn Greenwald with one request, set up encryption, and Greenwald kept putting it off.

## The Story
In late 2012, Greenwald received emails from an anonymous person calling himself **"Cincinnatus."** The writer said he had important material to share, but first Greenwald needed to install **PGP encryption** so they could communicate safely. He even sent instructions and a how-to video.

Greenwald describes how he meant to do it but found it technical and tedious, and kept delaying. With no proof the person had anything real, it never felt urgent. Cincinnatus, who turned out to be **Edward Snowden**, then reached the documentary filmmaker **Laura Poitras**, who took security seriously. Through Poitras, Greenwald was eventually brought back in. He admits that his reluctance to learn one small technical skill almost cost him the story that defined his career.

## The Lesson
Big opportunities often arrive looking like **small, annoying requests**. If you won't meet people on their terms, they'll find someone who will.

## When to Use This
- Someone promising asks you to adopt their tool or process before they'll engage
- You keep delaying a "boring" technical skill that's becoming essential
- A lead seems vague and you're unsure whether it's worth the effort
- You're wondering why opportunities go to others first

## Try This
- Keep a list of small skills (a tool, a security habit, a format) you've been putting off. Do one this week.
- When an unknown contact asks for a small, specific step, try doing it before deciding they aren't serious.
