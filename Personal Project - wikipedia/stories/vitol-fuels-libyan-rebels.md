# Vitol in Libya: Selling Fuel to a Rebellion

**One-line hook:** In the middle of a civil war, an oil trader agreed to supply fuel to rebels who had no government, no bank and no guarantee they would win.

## The Story
In 2011, as the uprising against Muammar Gaddafi spread across Libya, the rebels based in **Benghazi** faced a problem: they controlled some oil but had no way to sell it and no fuel to keep their fighters and cities running. Blas and Farchy describe how **Vitol**, the world's largest independent oil trader, stepped in. With political backing from Western governments and help from Qatar, Vitol supplied fuel to the rebels and agreed to be repaid in **crude oil** they could export later.

The deal was a huge gamble. If Gaddafi had won, Vitol might never have been paid. But it worked: the rebels won, and the authors argue the deal showed how traders can become **players in geopolitics**, not just neutral middlemen. They did what governments could not easily do themselves, and earned profit and influence for taking that risk.

## The Lesson
Being willing to act **when nobody else will provide a solution** can make you indispensable, but you have to understand and price the risk honestly.

## When to Use This
- A customer or partner is in crisis and every normal provider has walked away
- You're deciding whether to back an uncertain project early
- You're evaluating how private companies shape conflicts and politics
- You need creative payment terms when cash isn't available

## Try This
- When someone can't pay in cash, ask what else they have of value (future output, equity, access) and structure the deal around it.
- Before a big bet, write the "if we lose" scenario in one paragraph and decide if you can survive it.
