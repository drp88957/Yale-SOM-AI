# Paul Revere and the Rider Nobody Remembers

**One-line hook:** Two men rode out with the same urgent warning on the same night. Only one of them woke up the countryside.

## The Story
On the night of **April 18, 1775**, Paul Revere rode from Boston toward Lexington to warn that British troops were coming. Along the way he knocked on doors and alerted local militia leaders. The news spread fast, and by morning the colonists were ready to fight. It is one of the most famous word-of-mouth stories in American history.

What fewer people know is that a second man, **William Dawes**, set out that same night on a different route with **the same message**. But Dawes's ride did not spread the alarm nearly as well. Gladwell notes that fewer men from the towns along his route turned out to fight.

Why? Gladwell argues it was about **who** carried the message. Revere was a **Connector**: a sociable man who knew people across many social circles and knew exactly which doors to knock on. He was also a bit of a **Maven**, someone who gathered information. Dawes was an ordinary person with an ordinary network. Same message, very different messenger. This is Gladwell's **Law of the Few**: a small number of exceptional people (Connectors, Mavens, and Salesmen) drive most word-of-mouth epidemics.

## The Lesson
Ideas spread through **people, not just content**. Getting your message to the right few connectors matters more than reaching many people at random.

## When to Use This
- You're launching something and need word of mouth
- An important message at work isn't getting through
- You're trying to change opinions in a large group
- You're building your own professional network

## Try This
- Identify the two or three "Revere" people in your organization: the ones who know everyone. Brief them first.
- Map your network: who connects you to groups you don't normally reach?
