# "Act in Netflix's Best Interest": Removing the Rulebook

**One-line hook:** Netflix scrapped vacation tracking and most expense approvals, and replaced them with one sentence.

## The Story
Once Netflix had a team of strong performers who gave each other candid feedback, Hastings began **removing controls**. First went the vacation policy: there was no fixed number of days and no tracking. People could take time off when it made sense for them and their team. Then went most travel and expense approvals. The guidance was simply: **"Act in Netflix's best interest."** Spend company money as if it were your own.

Freedom didn't work by itself. Two things made it work. First, **leaders had to model it**: if the boss never takes a holiday, nobody else will either, so Hastings made a point of taking real vacations and talking about them. Second, managers had to **give context** (what's a sensible way to spend, when is a bad time to be away) and check spending afterward. When some people abused the freedom, Netflix dealt with it openly rather than bringing back rules for everyone, and in serious cases people were let go.

## The Lesson
**Rules are often there to manage a few weak performers.** With a strong team, clear context and visible role models work better than control, and people move faster.

## When to Use This
- Your team spends a lot of time getting approvals for small things
- You're designing policies for a growing team
- People don't take time off even though they're "allowed"
- You're deciding between more rules and better communication

## Try This
- Pick one approval step on your team and replace it with written guidance and an after-the-fact check.
- If you lead people, take visible time off and mention it, so others feel safe to do the same.
