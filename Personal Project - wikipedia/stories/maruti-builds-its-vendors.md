# Maruti Built Its Suppliers Instead of Just Buying From Them

**One-line hook:** You can't make a world-class car with second-rate parts, so Maruti helped create the parts makers.

## The Story
The government expected Maruti to make more and more of its car in India over time instead of importing parts. The problem was that Indian component makers at the time were not used to Japanese standards of **quality, cost and delivery**.

Rather than just squeezing suppliers on price, Maruti helped **build them up**. Bhargava describes how Maruti encouraged Indian firms to form **joint ventures with Japanese component makers** to get technology, and in many cases Maruti took **equity stakes in suppliers**. Suppliers were set up near the factory, shared quality expectations, and grew alongside Maruti.

The result was a supplier base that could keep up with Maruti's growth and standards. This network later helped India's whole auto components industry. Maruti treated suppliers as **long-term partners whose success was its own**, not as rivals to beat in each negotiation.

## The Lesson
When your weakest link is outside your company, **invest in the link**. Your system is only as strong as your suppliers.

## When to Use This
- Quality problems keep coming from vendors or partners
- Building operations in a market without a mature supply base
- Deciding between price-squeezing and long-term partnership
- Your team depends on another team that is struggling

## Try This
- Find your biggest outside bottleneck. Ask what training, tools or shared targets would fix it.
- Share forecasts and quality data with key partners so they can plan with you.
