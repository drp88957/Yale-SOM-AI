# How an American Supermarket Inspired Toyota's Pull System

**One-line hook:** Toyota's famous just-in-time system borrowed its key idea from grocery store shelves.

## The Story
In the years after World War II, Toyota was small, short on cash, and couldn't afford to fill warehouses with parts. American car companies used **mass production**: make huge batches and push them into inventory. **Taiichi Ohno** wanted a way to make only what was needed, when it was needed.

He found the idea in the **American supermarket**. A supermarket doesn't guess how much milk each customer will buy and push it into their homes. It keeps a small amount on the shelf, and when customers take items, the store **restocks just what was taken**. Ohno applied this inside the factory: each process "shops" for the parts it needs from the step before, and that step only makes enough to **replace what was pulled**. Simple cards called **kanban** signal what to make next.

This **pull system** cut inventory, exposed problems that piles of stock used to hide, and became a core part of the Toyota Production System.

## The Lesson
**Make things in response to real demand, not forecasts.** Extra inventory hides problems; less of it forces you to fix them.

## When to Use This
- Work piles up between teams and nobody knows what's really needed
- Your team produces reports, features, or content nobody uses yet
- You want a new idea and keep looking only inside your own industry
- Planning cycles are long and keep missing reality

## Try This
- Limit work-in-progress: only start a new task when the next person is ready to "pull" it.
- Look at a business in a totally different field and ask how it solves your exact problem.
