# Adam Rifkin and the Five-Minute Favor

**One-line hook:** A modest software entrepreneur became one of the best-connected people in Silicon Valley by doing small favors for anyone.

## The Story
Adam Rifkin is a Silicon Valley entrepreneur that Fortune named the best networker in its list. He isn't a smooth self-promoter; Grant describes him as shy and a "panda" rather than a "peacock." His secret is a simple habit: **the five-minute favor**. Rifkin believes you should be willing to do something that takes five minutes or less for anyone. Most often that means **making an introduction** between two people who could help each other, or sharing knowledge or feedback.

Over the years, these small acts built a huge network of people who knew him and trusted him. Grant also describes Rifkin reconnecting with "dormant ties", old contacts he hadn't spoken to in years. Such ties are valuable because they give you **new information** that your current circle doesn't have, while still offering the trust of an old relationship.

Grant contrasts this with takers who network to get something, often flattering people above them while treating people below poorly. Others can see through this, and their reputation catches up with them.

## The Lesson
**Networks built by giving last longer and reach further than networks built by asking.** Small, consistent favors add up.

## When to Use This
- You're job-hunting or building a career in a new field
- Networking feels fake or transactional to you
- You have contacts who could help each other but have never met
- You want to grow your reputation without self-promotion

## Try This
- This week, make one introduction between two people who could help each other, and ask nothing in return.
- Message two "dormant ties" you haven't spoken to in years and ask what they're working on.
