# How a Debate Champion Beat a Computer by Agreeing First

**One-line hook:** Facing an AI armed with mountains of evidence, a world-class debater won over the audience by starting with what he agreed with.

## The Story
In 2019, debate champion **Harish Natarajan** faced **IBM's Project Debater**, an AI system that could draw on a huge amount of research. The topic was whether preschools should be subsidized. The computer argued for subsidies; Harish argued against.

The AI came armed with a flood of studies and statistics. Harish didn't try to match it fact for fact. Instead, he **started by finding common ground**, acknowledging points where he and his opponent agreed, such as the importance of helping disadvantaged children. Then he focused on **a few strong arguments** rather than many. Audience votes suggested he moved more people toward his side.

Grant uses this to show how expert negotiators and debaters differ from average ones. Skilled persuaders **look for agreement, ask questions, and use fewer arguments**, because piling on reasons dilutes your best point and makes people defensive. Persuasion works less like a war and more like a **dance**.

## The Lesson
To change someone's mind, start with what you agree on, then make a few strong points instead of many weak ones.

## When to Use This
- Negotiating with someone who sees you as an opponent
- Pitching an idea to a skeptical boss or client
- Disagreeing with a teammate without damaging the relationship
- You're tempted to win by burying the other side in data

## Try This
- Open your next tough conversation by stating one thing you genuinely agree with them on.
- Cut your argument down to your two strongest reasons and drop the rest.
