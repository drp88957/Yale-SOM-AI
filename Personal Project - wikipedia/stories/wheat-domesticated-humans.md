# Did We Domesticate Wheat, or Did Wheat Domesticate Us?

**One-line hook:** Harari calls the Agricultural Revolution "history's biggest fraud" because farmers worked harder and ate worse than the foragers before them.

## The Story
We usually tell farming as a story of progress. Harari flips it. Foragers ate a varied diet, worked fewer hours, and faced fewer diseases. Early farmers spent their days clearing rocks, hauling water and weeding under the sun, with **backs, knees and diets that suffered**. Their food depended on one or two crops, so a drought or pest could mean famine.

So why did it happen? Harari argues that **wheat domesticated humans**, not the other way around. A wild grass spread across the world because humans served it. Each small step (a bit more planting, a slightly bigger village) seemed sensible on its own. But more food meant more children, and once the population grew there was **no going back** to foraging. He calls this the **luxury trap**: things meant to make life easier create new obligations, and soon we can't live without them.

## The Lesson
A string of reasonable small choices can lock you into a life (or a company) that's worse overall. **More output is not the same as a better life.**

## When to Use This
- You keep adding "efficiency" tools and somehow have less free time
- A growing business needs ever more effort just to stay in place
- You're upgrading your lifestyle and wondering what it will cost you to maintain
- A team adopts a process that's easy to start and very hard to undo

## Try This
- Before a "small upgrade," ask what new commitment it creates and whether you could reverse it.
- Measure success by the quality of daily life (yours or your team's), not only by total output.
