# Dabasir's Way Out: The 70-20-10 Plan for Debt

**One-line hook:** A former slave, buried in debt, climbed out by splitting every coin three ways.

## The Story
**Dabasir** the camel trader once lived beyond his means, borrowed too much, and ran away from his debts. He ended up a **slave** in Syria. In "The Camel Trader of Babylon," he explains the turning point: a woman asked whether he had the soul of a slave or a free man. He realized he had been running from his problems, and that **"where the determination is, the way can be found."** He returned to Babylon to face his creditors.

In "The Clay Tablets from Babylon," his plan is recorded on tablets. He divided every earning into three parts: **one-tenth to keep** as savings, **seven-tenths to live on** (no more, even if it meant simple living), and **two-tenths to pay creditors**, shared fairly among them. He told creditors honestly what he was doing and paid steadily.

Over time, he paid off every debt and also had savings left. Owning the problem changed how creditors treated him, too.

## The Lesson
Debt is beaten by a **simple, honest plan you stick to**, not by running from it. Even while paying off debt, keep a share for yourself.

## When to Use This
- Facing debt or overspending
- Avoiding a problem you're ashamed of
- Owning a mistake with people you've let down
- Needing a simple budget rule

## Try This
- Split income into save / live / repay buckets (Dabasir used 10/70/20). Automate it.
- Tell the people you owe (money or work) your plan and timeline honestly.
