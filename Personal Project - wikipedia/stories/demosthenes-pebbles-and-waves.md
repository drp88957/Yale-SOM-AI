# Demosthenes Trains His Voice with Pebbles

**One-line hook:** A sickly boy with a speech problem became the greatest speaker of ancient Athens.

## The Story
**Demosthenes** was born weak and sickly in ancient Athens, with a **speech impediment**. His father died when he was young, and the guardians who were supposed to protect his inheritance **stole it** instead.

He decided to become a great speaker so he could fight back in court. Holiday describes his famous training: he spoke with **pebbles in his mouth** to improve his diction, practiced speeches while running or out of breath, and spoke over the **roar of the waves** at the sea so his voice would be strong. He built an underground study and, to force himself to stay inside and practice, **shaved half his head** so he would be too embarrassed to go out.

Eventually he took his guardians to court and won. He went on to become one of the most famous orators in history. Holiday's point: the very weakness that held him back became the thing he **mastered**.

## The Lesson
Your weakness can become your strength if you **train directly against it**. Obstacles make the practice harder, and therefore make you better.

## When to Use This
- You're bad at a skill your career needs (speaking, writing, coding)
- You feel behind because of a weak start
- You're tempted to avoid the thing you struggle with
- You need to commit to a hard practice plan

## Try This
- Pick your biggest weakness and design a daily drill that targets it directly.
- Remove an escape route that lets you skip practice.
