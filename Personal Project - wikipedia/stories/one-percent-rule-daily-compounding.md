# The 1% Rule: Mastery Is Built on Boring Days

**One-line hook:** You don't need a dramatic breakthrough. You need to get slightly better today, and then again tomorrow.

## The Story
*Note: this page summarizes the book's core principle rather than a specific anecdote from it.*

Tommy Baker's central idea is that **lasting results come from small, consistent improvements**, roughly 1% at a time, rather than from big bursts of motivation. Most people chase the dramatic transformation, like a new program, a big resolution or a burst of intensity. When it fades, they quit. Baker argues for the opposite: commit to **showing up daily and improving slightly**, especially on the days when it feels pointless.

The challenge is that 1% gains are **invisible in the short term**. For a long time it looks as though nothing is happening, and that's when most people give up. Baker frames mastery as a **long game**. You have to fall in love with the process itself, not just the outcome, because the process is what you'll be living with most of the time.

As a simple illustration of the math (not a figure from the book): improving by 1% a day, compounded over a year, adds up to far more than a 365% gain, because each day builds on the previous one. Small steps, repeated, become large results.

## The Lesson
**Consistency beats intensity.** Commit to a small daily improvement and trust that it compounds, even when you can't see it yet.

## When to Use This
- You're starting a new skill and feel overwhelmed by how far you have to go
- You keep launching big plans that fizzle after a few weeks
- You're comparing yourself to people years ahead of you
- You're building a habit for your team, such as daily learning or small process fixes

## Try This
- Define the smallest daily action that moves you forward (for example, 15 minutes of practice) and track it on a calendar.
- Each week, ask: "What is one thing I can do 1% better next week?"
