# One Investment Beat All the Others Combined

**One-line hook:** At Thiel's venture fund, a single winner returned more than every other investment put together.

## The Story
Thiel explains that venture capital is ruled by the **power law**: results are not spread evenly. A tiny number of investments produce almost all the returns.

He describes how, at **Founders Fund**, the best investment in a fund can return more than **all the other investments combined**. In the fund that invested in **Facebook**, that single company beat the rest of the portfolio put together. Most startups in a typical portfolio fail or return little.

This leads to an unusual rule: **only invest in companies that could return the entire fund on their own.** Thiel says many investors miss this because they think about spreading bets evenly, as if outcomes were "normal." He then applies the idea to people (Ch. 6, "You Are Not a Lottery Ticket"): don't spread yourself thinly across many mediocre options hoping one pays off. Think hard about which path could matter most, and commit.

## The Lesson
In many areas, **a few things matter far more than everything else combined**. Spreading effort evenly can mean giving up the one bet that could change everything.

## When to Use This
- You're choosing which projects or clients to prioritize
- You're deciding whether to focus or keep many options open in your career
- You're reviewing a portfolio of investments or initiatives
- You're treating every task as equally important

## Try This
- For each current project, ask: "If this works, could it outweigh all the others?" Put your best hours there.
- Look back at the past year and identify the one or two things that drove most of your results.
