# The Beatles in Hamburg & the 10,000-Hour Rule

**One-line hook:** Before they were famous, the Beatles played eight-hour sets in Hamburg strip clubs, and that's where they got good.

## The Story
In the early 1960s, the Beatles were an ordinary band that got booked to play in **Hamburg, Germany**. Back home they were used to short sets. In Hamburg they had to play for **hours a night, many nights a week**. Over a few trips they played a huge number of live shows, more than most bands play in an entire career.

Gladwell connects this to research by psychologist **Anders Ericsson**: the top performers in a field had typically put in about **10,000 hours of practice**. He also tells the story of **Bill Gates**, who as a teenager in 1968 got rare access to a computer terminal at his school (Lakeside) and logged thousands of hours of programming before almost anyone else could.

The point: genius usually comes from **lots of practice made possible by an unusual opportunity**.

## The Lesson
Mastery takes huge amounts of practice, and *access to the chance to practice* is often the hidden advantage.

## When to Use This
- You feel behind compared to a "natural"
- You're planning how to build a deep skill
- You're judging someone's success and want to see the hidden opportunity behind it

## Try This
- Find your "Hamburg": a setting that forces many reps under pressure.
- When you admire someone, ask: "What unusual access did they get?"
