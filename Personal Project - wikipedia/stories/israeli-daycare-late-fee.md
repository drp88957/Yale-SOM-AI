# The Late Fee That Made Parents Later

**One-line hook:** A day-care center fined parents for picking up their kids late, and the number of late pickups went up.

## The Story
Levitt and Dubner describe a study of day-care centers in **Haifa, Israel**. Teachers had to stay after closing whenever parents came late, so economists tested a simple fix: a **small fine** (about $3) for any parent who arrived more than ten minutes late.

The result was the opposite of what anyone expected. Late pickups **rose** soon after the fine started, and they stayed high. The fine replaced a **moral incentive** (guilt about making a teacher wait) with an **economic one**, and the price was cheap. Parents could now buy their way out of guilt for a few dollars. Being late had become a service you paid for, not a wrong you did to someone.

Even after the fine was dropped, late pickups did not go back down. Once the old norm was broken, it stayed broken.

## The Lesson
An incentive does more than change a price. It changes **how people see the situation**. A small penalty can turn a moral duty into a cheap transaction and make behavior worse.

## When to Use This
- You're thinking about adding a penalty, fee or bonus to fix someone's behavior
- A team used to help out of goodwill, and you're about to start paying or fining for it
- A policy "should" have worked but the numbers moved the wrong way

## Try This
- Before adding a fine or reward, ask what *non-money* reason people already have to behave well, and whether you might be removing it.
- If you use a penalty, make it big enough to matter, or don't use one at all.
