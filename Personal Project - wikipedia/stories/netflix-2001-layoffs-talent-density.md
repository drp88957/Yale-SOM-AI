# The Layoff That Made Netflix Better

**One-line hook:** Netflix cut about a third of its staff in a crisis, and the smaller team got faster, happier and more creative.

## The Story
In 2001, after the dot-com bubble burst, Netflix was running out of money and had to lay off **roughly a third of its employees**. Reed Hastings expected the remaining people to be demoralized and overworked. He chose to keep the strongest performers and let go of the rest, including some who were pleasant but only adequate.

The opposite of what he feared happened. The office was **full of energy**. The smaller team got more done, faster, and enjoyed it more. Hastings concluded that the key factor was **talent density**: when everyone around you is excellent, people learn from each other, push each other, and need far fewer rules. A few mediocre performers drag down the whole group: they need extra management, lower the bar for discussions, and push the best people to leave.

This became the first building block of Netflix culture: increase talent density first, then increase candor, then remove controls.

## The Lesson
**A small team of excellent people often outperforms a bigger team with mixed performance.** Talent density is what makes freedom and speed possible.

## When to Use This
- You're deciding whether to hire one great person or two average ones
- A team feels slow and needs lots of process to function
- You're facing budget cuts and have to choose who stays
- Your best performers seem bored or are leaving

## Try This
- For your team, ask: "Which roles need a top performer to make everyone else better?"
- Before adding a new rule or process, ask whether the real fix is a stronger person in the role.
