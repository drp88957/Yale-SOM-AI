# Cirque du Soleil: A Circus Without Animals or Stars

**One-line hook:** In a dying industry, a group of street performers built a hit by removing the very things circuses were known for.

## The Story
By the 1980s the traditional circus was a **declining industry**. Kids had video games and other entertainment, animal-rights groups were turning the public against animal acts, and star performers were expensive. Circuses like Ringling Bros. fought each other for a shrinking audience by copying each other's acts.

**Cirque du Soleil**, started in Canada by former street performers, did not try to beat Ringling at its own game. It **eliminated** the costly parts of the circus: animal acts, star performers, and the three-ring format. It kept a few classic elements, like the tent, clowns, and acrobatics, but made them more refined. Then it **added** things from the theater: a storyline or theme for each show, artistic music and dance, and a more elegant setting.

The result appealed to a **new audience**: adults and corporate clients who would never have bought circus tickets, and who were willing to pay theater-level prices. The authors say Cirque reached revenue in a short time that took the leading traditional circus far longer to reach. It had created a **"blue ocean"**: uncontested market space where competition was irrelevant.

## The Lesson
Instead of fighting rivals in a crowded "red ocean," **change what the industry offers**. Ask what to eliminate, reduce, raise, and create, so you can cut costs and add new value at the same time.

## When to Use This
- Your industry is shrinking and everyone is copying each other
- You're competing against a much bigger player with the same offer
- You're designing a product or event and assume it must include "standard" features
- You want to attract people who currently don't buy at all

## Try This
- List the features your industry takes for granted. Pick two you could eliminate entirely.
- Borrow one element from a completely different industry and ask how it would change your offer.
