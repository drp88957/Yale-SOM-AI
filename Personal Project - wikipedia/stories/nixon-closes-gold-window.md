# The Night Nixon Cut the Dollar Loose From Gold

**One-line hook:** In one weekend in 1971, the U.S. ended the link between the dollar and gold and changed the world's money system.

## The Story
After World War II, the world's money system (known as **Bretton Woods**) was built around the U.S. dollar. Other currencies were tied to the dollar, and foreign governments could swap dollars for **gold** at a fixed price. The system made the dollar the center of global finance.

By the late 1960s, the U.S. was spending heavily, and it had issued far more dollars than it held gold. Other countries began asking for gold, and U.S. reserves shrank. In **August 1971**, President **Richard Nixon** announced that the U.S. would **stop exchanging dollars for gold**. He paired this with other emergency measures, including a temporary extra tax on imports. Rickards treats this "Nixon shock" as a key battle in the second currency war.

The result was a world of **floating currencies** backed by trust in governments rather than gold. The U.S. kept its central role, but other countries were left holding dollars whose value Washington could influence. Rickards sees this shift as a source of the currency tensions that followed for decades.

## The Lesson
Rules that seem permanent can be **changed suddenly** by the most powerful player when they stop serving its interests.

## When to Use This
- You depend on a partner or platform that controls the rules
- You're trying to understand why the dollar matters so much globally
- A powerful player's "commitment" underpins your plans

## Try This
- Identify the agreements your plans depend on and ask what happens if the stronger party walks away.
- Build in options so a sudden rule change doesn't leave you stuck.
