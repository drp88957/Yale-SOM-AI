# From Tembhli to a Billion: Scaling Aadhaar Through Partners

**One-line hook:** Aadhaar didn't scale by hiring a giant government army; it scaled by letting many partners do the enrolling.

## The Story
The first Aadhaar numbers were handed out in **2010** in **Tembhli**, a small tribal village in Maharashtra. Starting in a poor, rural place was symbolic. Aadhaar was meant to give an identity to people who had often been left out of the system.

The bigger challenge was **scale**: enrolling hundreds of millions of people across a huge and diverse country. Instead of building one large government machine to do every enrollment, the authority (UIDAI) set **standards and technology**, and let an **ecosystem of "registrars"** do the work. These included state governments, banks and other agencies, which hired enrollment agencies on the ground. UIDAI kept a small core team and focused on the central system, especially **de-duplication** through biometrics.

This approach let enrollment grow fast, with many players running in parallel. The authors present it as a model for building public systems: **a thin core with open standards, and many partners at the edge.**

## The Lesson
To scale fast, **don't do everything yourself**. Own the standards and the core, and let partners handle the rest.

## When to Use This
- Your team is the bottleneck for a big rollout
- Designing a platform others will build on
- Deciding what to own and what to outsource
- Launching something symbolic to show who it's for

## Try This
- Split your project into "core we must control" and "edge others can do." Write clear standards for the edge.
- Choose a first launch location or user that sends a message about who the work is for.
