# Kunwer Sachdev: Finding a Business in Broken Inverters

**One-line hook:** He went from selling pens to running a cable TV business, then built an inverter company because the ones he used kept failing.

## The Story
**Kunwer Sachdev** did not follow a straight path. He grew up in Delhi in a modest family and started out in sales, including **selling pens**. He then moved into the **cable TV** business, where he learned about electronics and dealing with equipment.

In Delhi, **power cuts** were common, and many homes used inverters for backup. Sachdev noticed that the inverters available were often **poor quality** and broke down. Instead of just complaining, he saw it as an opportunity. Using what he had learned, he began making his own inverters and built **Su-Kam**, which grew into a major Indian power backup brand.

Bansal uses his story to show how entrepreneurs without formal business training often **connect the dots** from earlier, seemingly unrelated jobs. Sales taught him customers; cable TV taught him electronics; daily frustration showed him the problem.

## The Lesson
Your next opportunity is often **a product you're frustrated with**. Earlier "random" jobs give you the skills to fix it.

## When to Use This
- You feel your career path looks scattered
- Searching for a business or project idea
- You keep running into a poor-quality product or service
- Deciding whether a side skill is "wasted"

## Try This
- List three products or services that annoy you every week. Which could you improve?
- Write down skills from each past job, and look for a combination few people have.
