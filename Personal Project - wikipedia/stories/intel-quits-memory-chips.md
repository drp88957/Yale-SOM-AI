# Intel Walks Away From the Business It Invented

**One-line hook:** Intel was built on memory chips, and its survival depended on abandoning them.

## The Story
In its early years Intel's main business was **DRAM memory chips**. But in the 1980s, Japanese firms flooded the market with high-quality, low-cost memory. Miller describes how American chipmakers were losing badly on both price and quality, and Silicon Valley feared for its future.

Under **Andy Grove** and **Gordon Moore**, Intel made a painful decision: it **exited the DRAM business**, the product that had defined the company, and bet instead on **microprocessors**, the "brains" of personal computers. The move meant layoffs and closing factories. But microprocessors turned out to be a far better business. Intel's chips became the standard inside PCs, and the company that nearly lost to Japan became one of the most powerful firms in tech. Grove's management style, driven by constant fear of being overtaken, became famous.

## The Lesson
Your identity can become a trap. When you are losing a fight you can't win, **the bravest move may be to leave it** and put everything into the area where you can lead.

## When to Use This
- Your original product or skill is being commoditised by cheaper rivals
- You're holding on to a project mainly because "it's who we are"
- You need to decide whether to cut a team, product line or career path
- A competitor is beating you on price and you can't match them

## Try This
- Ask: "If a new leader took over tomorrow with no emotional attachment, what would they stop doing?" Then consider doing it yourself.
- List where you are clearly **better**, not just present. Move resources there.
