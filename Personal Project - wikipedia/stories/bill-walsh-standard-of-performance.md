# Bill Walsh: Focus on the Standard, Not the Score

**One-line hook:** He took over the worst team in football and started with how people answered the phone.

## The Story
When **Bill Walsh** became head coach of the **San Francisco 49ers**, the team was one of the worst in the league. The obvious move was to promise a quick turnaround and big wins.

Holiday describes how Walsh did the opposite. He focused on a **"Standard of Performance"**: a detailed set of expectations for how everyone in the organization should act and work. It covered not just players and coaches, but small details like how staff answered phones, how offices were kept, and how people carried themselves. He cared more about doing things the right way, every day, than about short-term results.

The early results were not great. But the standards built a culture of **excellence and discipline**, and within a few seasons the 49ers won the Super Bowl. Holiday's point is that ego wants instant success and credit. Walsh trusted the **process**, and the results followed.

## The Lesson
**Stop obsessing over the scoreboard and fix the standards.** When the daily process is right, results come.

## When to Use This
- Taking over a struggling team or project
- Pressure for fast wins is pushing you to cut corners
- Culture feels sloppy in small ways
- You're judging yourself only by outcomes

## Try This
- Write a short "standard of performance" for your team: 5–7 behaviors everyone should do daily.
- Track whether you met the standard this week, not just whether you hit the target.
