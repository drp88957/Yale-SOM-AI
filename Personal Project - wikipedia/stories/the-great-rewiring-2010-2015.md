# The Great Rewiring: What Changed Around 2012

**One-line hook:** Haidt shows graph after graph where teen mental health is fairly stable for years and then suddenly bends upward in the early 2010s.

## The Story
Haidt calls the period from roughly **2010 to 2015** the **"Great Rewiring of Childhood."** In those years, teens moved from basic flip phones to **smartphones** with front-facing cameras, constant internet access and social media apps like Instagram. Childhood shifted from being play-based to being phone-based.

He presents data from the US and several other countries showing that rates of teen **anxiety, depression and self-harm** rose sharply around the early 2010s, especially among girls. He argues the timing points to phones and social media as a major cause, since they arrived at the same moment across many countries. Haidt addresses critics who say this is only correlation, arguing that experiments, the timing and the mechanisms together make a strong case. Other researchers dispute how large the effect is, and Haidt acknowledges the debate while maintaining his conclusion.

## The Lesson
When many trends **turn at the same moment**, look for what changed at that moment. And when data is debated, weigh timing, mechanism and experiments together.

## When to Use This
- You see a sudden shift in a metric and need to find the cause
- You're deciding family rules around phones and social media
- You're debating whether a correlation in your data is causal
- You're making the case for a policy change at a school or company

## Try This
- When a chart bends, list everything that changed just before the bend. Test the strongest candidates.
- Look for the same pattern in different groups or places; if it repeats, the cause is more likely real.
