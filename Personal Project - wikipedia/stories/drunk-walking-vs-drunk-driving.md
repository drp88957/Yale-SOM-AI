# Should Your Drunk Friend Walk Home?

**One-line hook:** Per mile traveled, walking home drunk turns out to be far more deadly than driving drunk.

## The Story
At the start of SuperFreakonomics, Levitt and Dubner pose a party dilemma. Your friend has had too much to drink. The responsible choice seems obvious: don't drive, **walk home**.

Then they do the math. Drunk driving gets all the attention, and rightly so, because it kills many people. But they look at deaths **per mile** traveled. Drunk pedestrians stray into roads, lie down in streets and get hit. When you compare the risk per mile, a drunk walker is **much more likely to die** than a drunk driver (the book puts it at several times higher). The authors are clear that this is **not** an argument for drunk driving, which also endangers others. Their point is that the "safe" choice was never checked against data.

The real answer is a third option: a sober ride, a taxi, or staying where you are. The comparison only looked like a choice between two options.

## The Lesson
The option that **feels** safe is not always the one that **is** safe. Compare risks on the same basis (per mile, per use, per dollar) before judging.

## When to Use This
- You're picking the "safe" option without seeing its numbers
- Two risks are compared using totals instead of rates
- A decision seems to have only two choices

## Try This
- Put the risks on the same scale: per use, per hour, per customer, per dollar.
- Look for a third option before accepting a forced choice between two bad ones.
