# Sabse Sasta Din: When the Sale Was Too Successful

**One-line hook:** A one-day sale drew such huge crowds that stores struggled to cope.

## The Story
Big Bazaar created **"Sabse Sasta Din"** ("the cheapest day"), a big sale tied to **Republic Day**. The idea linked a national holiday, when families were free, to deep discounts on everyday goods.

The response was far bigger than expected. **Huge crowds** turned up, and in some places stores had trouble managing the rush. Biyani tells this as proof that Indian shoppers, including many who had never been to a modern store, would come in large numbers when the offer matched their love of a good deal and a festive outing.

The event became a regular fixture and helped make Big Bazaar a household name. Biyani's point was that **shopping in India is social and emotional**, tied to festivals and occasions, not just a chore. Retail that rides on those occasions can create moments people talk about.

## The Lesson
Tie your offer to **moments people already care about**. A good deal plus a shared occasion can create demand far larger than the deal alone. But plan for success, too.

## When to Use This
- Planning a launch or promotion
- Choosing the timing of a big announcement
- Trying to make a brand known quickly
- Preparing operations for a demand spike

## Try This
- List the holidays, seasons or rituals your customers already celebrate. Plan one offer around them.
- Before a big push, ask "What breaks if three times as many people show up?" and fix it.
