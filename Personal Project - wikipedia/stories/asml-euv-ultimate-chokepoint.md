# One Machine, One Company: ASML and the Most Complex Tool Ever Sold

**One-line hook:** The world's most advanced chips depend on a machine that fires lasers at tiny drops of tin, and only one company on Earth can build it.

## The Story
To print the smallest features on modern chips, manufacturers need **extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography**. Miller describes how hard this was to achieve. The machine creates EUV light by blasting **tiny droplets of tin with a powerful laser**, again and again, many thousands of times per second, producing a plasma far hotter than normal industrial processes. The light is then bounced off mirrors so smooth they are among the most precise objects ever made.

Developing EUV took decades and huge investment. In the end, only one company, the Dutch firm **ASML**, succeeded. It relies on a global web of specialist suppliers, such as Germany's Zeiss for optics and US firms for the light source. Miller shows how this made EUV a perfect **chokepoint**: because the US and its allies control key parts of the supply chain, they were able to block China from buying these machines, limiting its ability to make the most advanced chips.

## The Lesson
The most powerful position in a system is often a **single, irreplaceable step** that everyone else depends on. Find those chokepoints, both to understand your risks and to see where real power lies.

## When to Use This
- You depend on a single supplier, platform or vendor for something critical
- You're analysing tech competition between the US and China
- You're looking for a defensible position in an industry
- You're stress-testing a supply chain for risk

## Try This
- Map your key workflow and mark any step with only **one** possible provider. Those are your chokepoints; plan a backup.
- Ask where in your industry you could become the hard-to-replace step.
