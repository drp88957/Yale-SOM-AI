# Peugeot Doesn't Exist (and Yet It Builds Cars)

**One-line hook:** A car company has no body, no address it can't move, and no single owner, yet thousands of strangers work together for it every day.

## The Story
Harari asks a simple question: where exactly is **Peugeot**? It isn't the factories, which could be torn down. It isn't the workers, who could all be fired. It isn't the cars, which could all be scrapped. Peugeot is a **legal fiction**, a "limited liability company" that exists because lawyers, courts and people agree to believe in it.

His bigger point is that this ability to believe in **shared stories** is what made *Homo sapiens* different. Chimps can cooperate closely, but only with animals they know personally. Gossip lets humans manage groups of maybe 150. Beyond that, strangers cooperate only because they believe the **same myths**: gods, nations, money, human rights, companies. None of these exist outside our shared imagination, and all of them let millions of strangers work together.

## The Lesson
Large groups don't run on personal relationships; they run on **stories everyone believes**. If you want people who don't know each other to cooperate, give them a shared story worth believing.

## When to Use This
- Your team grows past the size where everyone knows everyone
- A merger or reorg leaves people unsure what "we" stand for
- You're trying to rally strangers (users, volunteers, investors) around an idea
- You're puzzled why a brand or institution holds power far beyond its assets

## Try This
- Write down the one-sentence story your team or project tells about itself. If you can't, people are probably believing different ones.
- When scaling a group, invest in shared rituals and language, not just more meetings.
