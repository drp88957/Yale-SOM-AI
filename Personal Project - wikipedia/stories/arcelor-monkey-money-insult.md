# "Monkey Money": How Arcelor's Insults Backfired on Its Own Boss

**One-line hook:** The CEO of Europe's steel champion sneered at an Indian-born rival's bid, and the sneer became a weapon against him.

## The Story
In early 2006, **Lakshmi Mittal**, whose Mittal Steel had grown by buying and turning around struggling steel plants around the world, launched a **hostile bid for Arcelor**, the European steel giant formed from French, Luxembourg, and Spanish companies. Much of the offer was in Mittal Steel shares.

Arcelor's CEO, **Guy Dollé**, reacted with open contempt. He dismissed the share part of the offer as **"monnaie de singe"**, "monkey money," and suggested Arcelor was a refined product while Mittal's company was a cheaper one. Politicians in France and Luxembourg also lined up against the bid. To many observers, the tone suggested that **an Indian-born outsider** wasn't seen as a fit owner for a European champion.

The book shows how this backfired. The remarks made Arcelor's leadership look arrogant, and **shareholders**, who cared about value, not national pride, grew more open to Mittal. Mittal stayed calm, kept talking to investors, and improved his offer. In the end, Arcelor agreed to the deal, creating **ArcelorMittal**.

## The Lesson
**Contempt is a bad negotiating strategy.** Insulting the other side can turn neutral parties against you, while staying calm and focused on value wins them over.

## When to Use This
- You're up against an established player who looks down on you
- You're tempted to attack a rival's credibility instead of their offer
- You're negotiating across cultures or nationalities
- You need to win over a third party (investors, a board, voters)

## Try This
- In your next tough negotiation, focus every argument on value to the decision-makers, not on the other side's flaws.
- If someone dismisses you, respond with facts and patience, and let their tone speak for itself.
