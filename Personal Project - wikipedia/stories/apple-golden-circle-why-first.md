# Apple Doesn't Sell Computers First. It Sells a Belief.

**One-line hook:** Rewrite Apple's pitch in the order most companies use and it suddenly sounds like everyone else.

## The Story
Sinek's **Golden Circle** has three rings: **Why** (your purpose or belief) in the center, then **How** (your process), then **What** (your products). Most organizations communicate from the outside in: "Here is what we make, here's how it's better, want to buy it?"

Sinek shows what a typical computer company pitch sounds like: "We make great computers. They're beautifully designed, simple to use and user friendly. Want to buy one?" It is fine, but forgettable. Then he rewrites it the way Apple communicates, inside out: **"Everything we do, we believe in challenging the status quo. We believe in thinking differently."** The way Apple does this is by making beautifully designed, simple products. "We just happen to make great computers. Want to buy one?"

Same facts, but a very different feeling. Because people buy into Apple's **why**, they will accept Apple selling them phones and music players. Sinek contrasts this with Dell, which tried to sell things like MP3 players and PDAs and struggled, because people saw Dell as a company that makes computers, not one that stands for something.

## The Lesson
**People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it.** Lead with your belief, and your products become proof of it.

## When to Use This
- You're pitching an idea and listing features that don't excite anyone
- You're writing a mission, a cover letter, or a personal introduction
- Your company wants to move into a new product area
- Your team is busy but can't explain the point of their work

## Try This
- Rewrite your next pitch to open with one sentence of belief ("We believe...") before any features.
- Ask your team to each write the company's "why" in one line, then compare answers.
