# The YouTube Engineer Who Watched His Algorithm Go Down the Rabbit Hole

**One-line hook:** A former YouTube engineer built software to see what the recommendation system was actually promoting, and didn't like what he found.

## The Story
**Guillaume Chaslot** worked as an engineer at YouTube on its recommendation system. Fisher describes how Chaslot became worried that the system was optimised for one main goal: **watch time**. Whatever kept people watching longer got recommended more. When he raised concerns internally, he felt they didn't go anywhere.

After leaving, Chaslot built a program to track **what YouTube actually recommended** to viewers. He found that the system often steered people toward more extreme, sensational and conspiratorial videos, because those kept people watching. Fisher uses this and other research to argue that YouTube's algorithm acted like a **rabbit hole**, pushing viewers from ordinary content toward fringe content, without any human choosing to do so. YouTube disputed parts of these claims and later made changes to its system.

## The Lesson
You don't really know what your system does until you **measure its outputs from the outside**. A single metric like "watch time" can quietly reshape behaviour in ways nobody planned.

## When to Use This
- You run a recommendation, ranking or incentive system
- Your dashboards show the target metric, but not its side effects
- An insider is raising concerns that leadership dismisses
- You're trying to understand how people get radicalised online

## Try This
- Audit your system from the user's side: experience what it actually serves up, not just the summary numbers.
- Track at least one "health" metric alongside every growth metric.
