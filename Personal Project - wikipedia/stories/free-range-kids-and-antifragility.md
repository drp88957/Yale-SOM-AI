# The Mom Who Let Her Son Ride the Subway Alone

**One-line hook:** When Lenore Skenazy let her 9-year-old ride the New York subway alone, she was called "America's worst mom," and Haidt thinks she was right.

## The Story
Haidt argues that children are **antifragile**: like muscles or immune systems, they need challenges, risks and small failures to grow strong. Kids who climb trees, play unsupervised and sort out their own arguments learn to handle fear and setbacks. But in recent decades parents, worried about rare dangers, increasingly kept children indoors and closely supervised. Haidt calls this a shift from a **"discover mode"** of exploration to a **"defend mode"** of constant caution.

He highlights **Lenore Skenazy**, a journalist who let her young son ride the subway home alone and wrote about it. The backlash was enormous. Skenazy went on to start the **Free-Range Kids** movement, and later co-founded **Let Grow** with Haidt and others, an organisation that encourages schools and parents to give children more independence, such as walking to school or running errands on their own.

## The Lesson
Protecting people from every difficulty makes them **more fragile, not safer**. Growth comes from manageable risk and the chance to recover from small failures.

## When to Use This
- You're a parent deciding how much freedom to give your child
- You manage new team members and are tempted to shield them from every hard task
- You notice yourself avoiding small risks that would help you grow
- A school or organisation is adding rules that remove all challenge

## Try This
- Give a child (or a junior colleague) one real responsibility this week that slightly stretches them, and don't rescue them too quickly.
- Ask: "Is this risk actually dangerous, or just uncomfortable?"
