# Belichick's Start: Do the Work Nobody Wants

**One-line hook:** One of football's greatest coaches started by breaking down game film that nobody else wanted to watch.

## The Story
Before he became a famous head coach, **Bill Belichick** began his NFL career in a very junior role with the **Baltimore Colts**, for almost no pay. Holiday describes how he made himself useful by doing the work others avoided, especially **breaking down game film** for hours to find patterns that coaches could use.

He did not push to be noticed or demand credit. Instead he made the people above him look good. Over time, his analysis became so valuable that coaches relied on him, and his career grew from there.

Holiday calls this the **"canvas strategy"**: find the people above you and clear the path for them. Help them do their work better, offer ideas, take the boring tasks. In the short term you give up credit; in the long term you gain skill, trust and relationships that no amount of self-promotion could buy.

## The Lesson
Early in your career, **be useful, not impressive**. Making others succeed is the fastest way to learn and the surest way to be trusted.

## When to Use This
- You're new on a team and want to stand out
- You feel underused or unnoticed
- You want to learn from someone senior
- You're tempted to fight for credit

## Try This
- Ask your manager: "What's a task that eats your time that I could take off your plate?"
- Pick one unglamorous job (data cleanup, notes, analysis) and do it better than anyone expects.
