# Herbie and the Scout Hike: Your Team Is Only as Fast as Its Slowest Hiker

**One-line hook:** A weekend hike with a troop of Boy Scouts teaches a plant manager more about his factory than any MBA.

## The Story
Plant manager Alex Rogo reluctantly leads his son's Boy Scout troop on an overnight hike. The boys walk single file, and the line keeps **spreading out**. The kids in front race ahead, and gaps open up behind a chubby, slow boy named **Herbie**. Alex realizes the speed of the whole troop is set by Herbie, no matter how fast the boys up front walk. The others hurrying ahead only makes the line longer, the same way extra work-in-progress piles up in his plant.

Alex makes two moves. First, he puts **Herbie at the front of the line**, so nobody can get ahead of him and the troop stays together. Second, he opens Herbie's backpack and finds it stuffed with heavy snacks and gear he didn't need to carry alone. He **spreads Herbie's load across the other boys**. Herbie speeds up, and the whole troop arrives faster.

Back at the plant, Alex starts hunting for his "Herbies": the machines that set the pace for everything.

## The Lesson
**A system's output is set by its constraint.** Speeding up anything else just builds piles of waiting work. Find the Herbie, let him set the pace, and take load off him.

## When to Use This
- A project is late even though "everyone is busy"
- One person or approval step keeps holding up the team
- You want to speed up a process and don't know where to start
- Teams are being judged on local speed instead of overall flow

## Try This
- Map your process and ask: "Where does work wait the longest?" That is your Herbie.
- List what your Herbie does that someone else could do, and move it off their plate.
