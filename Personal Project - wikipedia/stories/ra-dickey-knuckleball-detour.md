# R.A. Dickey: Going Backward to Get Unstuck

**One-line hook:** A pitcher's career was falling apart until he learned a pitch that made him worse before it made him great.

## The Story
R.A. Dickey was a highly drafted pitcher with a promising future. Then a medical exam revealed he was **missing a key ligament in his throwing elbow**. His signing bonus was cut dramatically. For years he bounced between the minor leagues and the majors as an average pitcher, and his career was heading nowhere.

So he took a **detour**. He committed to learning the **knuckleball**, an unusual, slow pitch that floats unpredictably. It was a strange choice for a pitcher who already had a career to protect. At first he got *worse*. He had to go back to basics, seek out experienced knuckleballers for advice, and accept results that looked like failure. Eventually it clicked. Dickey became one of the best pitchers in baseball and won the **Cy Young Award**, the sport's top pitching honor.

Grant uses Dickey's story to explain how to get unstuck on a plateau. Sometimes progress requires **taking a step back**. You leave a local peak, accept a dip in performance, and climb toward a higher one. Most people refuse the detour because the short-term drop feels like failure.

## The Lesson
When you're stuck, the path forward may **go downhill first**. Be willing to look worse temporarily to become much better.

## When to Use This
- Your career or skill has plateaued despite effort
- You're considering a major change of method or role
- A team resists a new process because performance dips at first
- You've suffered a setback that closed your original path

## Try This
- Ask: "If I started over on this skill, what would I do differently?" Then test it on low-stakes reps.
- Find a mentor who has already taken the detour you're considering.
