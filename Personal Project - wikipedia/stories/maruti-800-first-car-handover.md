# The Maruti 800 and the Car India Was Waiting For

**One-line hook:** India had waited decades for a modern, affordable car, and when it came, demand was far bigger than supply.

## The Story
Before Maruti, Indian buyers had only a few old car models to choose from. They were costly, outdated, and often had long waiting lists. Many people thought ordinary Indian families could not afford a car, so there was no market for one.

Maruti bet the other way. It launched the **Maruti 800**, a small, fuel-efficient, modern car priced for the middle class. In December 1983, Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi** handed over the keys of the first car to a buyer, **Harpal Singh**. Bookings were so large that cars were allotted through a **draw**, because demand far exceeded production.

Bhargava shows that the "no market" belief was wrong. There was **hidden demand** that old products had never served. Once a good product came at the right price, families who had never thought about a car became buyers. Maruti then grew production and a supplier network to match the demand it had found.

## The Lesson
"There's no market" often means **"nobody has offered the right product at the right price yet."** Weak demand for bad options says little about demand for a good one.

## When to Use This
- Someone says customers "won't pay" for something new
- An industry has few choices and long waits
- Deciding whether to price for the mass market
- Launching a product into a market that looks small

## Try This
- Ask: "Is demand low, or are current options just poor?" Look for long waits, complaints and workarounds.
- Design for the price your target customer can pay, then work backward to the product.
