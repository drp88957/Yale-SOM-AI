# Three Air Mattresses and a Rent Problem: How Airbnb Began

**One-line hook:** Two designers who couldn't pay their rent noticed that every hotel in town was sold out.

## The Story
In 2007, **Brian Chesky** and **Joe Gebbia**, two friends from the Rhode Island School of Design, were sharing an apartment in San Francisco and struggling to pay the rent. A big **design conference** was coming to the city, and the hotels were booked up.

They had an idea: blow up some **air mattresses** on their floor and rent them to conference visitors, with breakfast included. They made a simple website, called it something like "Air Bed & Breakfast," and got **three guests**. The guests were different people from different backgrounds, and the experience went well. The founders realized they had earned real money and, more importantly, that **strangers were willing to stay in someone's home**.

They later brought in **Nathan Blecharczyk**, a former roommate of Gebbia's, as the technical co-founder. The early years were hard and many investors passed. But the seed was planted by solving their own problem in a moment when demand far outran supply.

## The Lesson
**Big ideas often start as a scrappy fix to your own problem.** Look for moments when demand is clearly bigger than supply, and test a tiny version right away.

## When to Use This
- You're looking for a business or side-project idea
- You have a personal problem that others probably share
- You want to test an idea without building much
- You see a market that's overloaded at certain moments

## Try This
- List three problems you've had this month and ask who else has them.
- Run a weekend-sized test of your idea with real people paying real money.
