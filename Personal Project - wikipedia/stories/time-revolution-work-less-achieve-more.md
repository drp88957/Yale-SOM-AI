# The Time Revolution: Most of Your Results Come From a Sliver of Your Time

**One-line hook:** Koch argues we don't have too little time; we waste most of it on things that barely matter.

## The Story
Koch applies the 80/20 Principle to personal life in what he calls a **"time revolution."** His claim is that **most of what we achieve comes from a small fraction of our time**, while most of our hours produce little of value. The problem is not a shortage of time, but how badly it is spent.

He is critical of standard **time management**, which tries to squeeze more tasks into each day. Koch says that just makes people busier at low-value activities. Instead, he suggests a more radical approach: identify the few activities that produce most of your happiness and achievement, **do much more of them**, and **cut back hard** on the rest, even if that feels uncomfortable or unconventional. He encourages readers to think about the activities where they produce unusually good results with relatively little effort, and to build their work and life around those.

Koch also describes being somewhat **"lazy" in a smart way**: thinking more and acting less, focusing on the few things that really move the needle rather than working longer hours.

## The Lesson
**Effort and reward are not linked evenly.** Find the small set of activities that create most of your value, and protect time for them ruthlessly.

## When to Use This
- You're working long hours and still feel behind
- Your to-do list is long and everything looks urgent
- You're planning your career and want to know where to focus
- You're learning a new skill and feel overwhelmed by everything to cover

## Try This
- Track your time for one week, then mark the few hours that produced your best results or most joy.
- Pick one low-value recurring activity and stop doing it (or delegate it) for a month. See if anyone notices.
