# Amazon Got Investors to Pay It Not to Make Profits

**One-line hook:** For years Amazon reported thin profits, yet investors kept rewarding it, because Bezos sold a story worth more than earnings.

## The Story
Galloway points out an unusual advantage Amazon had for most of its history: **access to cheap capital**. Most companies are punished by investors if they don't show profits. Amazon reported **very thin or no profits** for years, yet its stock value kept rising.

The reason, Galloway argues, is **the story**. Jeff Bezos told a clear, bold vision: Amazon would be the "everything store," investing for the long term in customers, warehouses, logistics, and new businesses. Investors bought into that vision and were willing to wait. That meant Amazon could pour money into things competitors couldn't afford, such as faster delivery, **Prime**, cloud computing with **AWS**, and lower prices, while rivals like traditional retailers had to answer to shareholders demanding profits now.

Galloway calls this kind of patient money **"visionary capital,"** and lists it among the factors that make a company capable of becoming a trillion-dollar giant. A compelling story, he argues, can be a company's most valuable asset because it lowers the cost of money and buys time.

## The Lesson
**A credible long-term story can buy you time and resources** your rivals don't get. Investors, bosses, and partners fund visions they believe in.

## When to Use This
- You need support for a long-term project that won't pay off soon
- You're competing against a rival with deeper pockets
- You're pitching investors or leadership for funding
- You're wondering why a money-losing company is valued so highly

## Try This
- Write your project's story in three sentences: where you're going, why it matters, and what the next milestone proves.
- Before asking for budget, show early evidence that the story is working.
