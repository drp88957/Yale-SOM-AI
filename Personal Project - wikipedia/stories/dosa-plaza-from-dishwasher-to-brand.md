# Prem Ganapathy: From Washing Dishes to Dosa Plaza

**One-line hook:** He arrived in Mumbai with almost nothing and was cheated on day one; years later he ran a dosa chain.

## The Story
**Prem Ganapathy** came from a village in Tamil Nadu to Mumbai as a teenager, promised a job by someone who then **abandoned him** with no money in a strange city. He did not speak the local language. He took whatever work he could find, including **washing dishes** at a bakery and small restaurants, and saved bit by bit.

He then started a small **dosa cart** on the street. What set him apart was not just hard work but **experimenting**: he kept trying new dosa varieties, often using ideas from customers and other cuisines, and built a much larger menu than a typical dosa stall. His focus on cleanliness and variety drew a steady crowd, including college students.

From the cart he moved to a small outlet, and then built **Dosa Plaza** into a chain with many outlets. Rashmi Bansal shows that he had no degree or business training, only persistence, learning by doing, and a willingness to keep improving his product.

## The Lesson
You don't need a perfect start. **Begin small, keep experimenting, and let customers shape the product.** Steady small gains compound.

## When to Use This
- You feel behind because others had better starting points
- You're waiting for the "right" moment to start
- A small business or project feels stuck
- You've had a big setback early on

## Try This
- Start the smallest version of your idea this week, even if it's a "cart" version.
- Ask your users what they'd want next, and test one new idea every week.
