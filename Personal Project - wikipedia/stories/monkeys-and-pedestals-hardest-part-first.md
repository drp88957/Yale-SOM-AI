# Train the Monkey Before You Build the Pedestal

**One-line hook:** If your plan is a monkey reciting Shakespeare on a pedestal, don't start by building the pedestal.

## The Story
Varol shares a way of thinking from **Astro Teller**, who leads **X**, Google's "moonshot factory." Imagine your goal is to get a **monkey to stand on a pedestal and recite Shakespeare**. Where should you start?

Most teams start with the **pedestal**. It's easy and lets you show progress to your boss. But the pedestal was never the hard part. All the risk is in **training the monkey**. If the monkey can't be trained, the pedestal is wasted effort. X's motto captures it: tackle the monkey first.

At X, teams are encouraged to find the **hardest, most uncertain part** of a project early and try to kill the idea quickly. If it survives, it's worth continuing. Varol connects this to how rocket scientists work: rather than polishing the easy parts, find the **riskiest assumption** and test it first. Teams are even rewarded for shutting down projects that won't work, so people aren't afraid to admit early that the monkey can't talk.

## The Lesson
Do the hardest, most uncertain part first. Easy progress on the safe parts creates the illusion of momentum while the real risk goes untested.

## When to Use This
- Kicking off a new project, product, or startup
- Your team reports lots of progress, but the core risk hasn't been tested
- Planning a roadmap and deciding what to build first
- You're avoiding the scary part of your work

## Try This
- Ask your team: "What's our monkey?" Name the single riskiest assumption and test it this week.
- Celebrate killing a bad idea early as a win, not a failure.
