# Schwab's Chalk "6": Motivating a Team Without Saying a Word

**One-line hook:** A steel boss wrote one number on the mill floor, and the workers started competing to beat it.

## The Story
Charles Schwab, a famous steel executive, had a mill manager whose workers weren't meeting their output targets. The manager had tried coaxing, pushing and threatening. Nothing worked.

At the end of the day shift, Schwab asked a worker how many **"heats"** (batches of steel) the shift had made that day. "Six." Schwab took a piece of chalk, **wrote a big "6" on the floor**, and walked away without a word. When the night shift came in, they asked what it meant. On seeing it the next morning, the day-shift workers found that the night shift had rubbed out the 6 and written a **7**. The day shift then pushed to beat that. Before long, the mill that had been lagging was producing more than the company's other plants.

Schwab's explanation: the way to get things done is to **stimulate competition**, not in a cut-throat way, but through the desire to excel. Carnegie's principle: **"Throw down a challenge."**

## The Lesson
**A visible score and a worthy challenge can motivate more than orders or threats.** People want to prove themselves.

## When to Use This
- Your team is stuck and pep talks aren't working
- Work is repetitive and progress feels invisible
- You're trying to improve a key metric
- Two groups do similar work and could learn from each other

## Try This
- Put one key number somewhere everyone can see it, updated daily, with no lecture attached.
- Frame a goal as a challenge ("Can we beat last week?") rather than a demand.
