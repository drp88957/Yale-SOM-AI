# Real-Estate Agents Sell Their Own Homes Differently

**One-line hook:** When agents sell their own houses, they wait longer and get more money than when they sell yours.

## The Story
A real-estate agent seems to share your goal: a higher price means a bigger commission. But Levitt runs the numbers. The agent personally keeps only a **small slice** of the commission, so an extra $10,000 on the sale price might put only around $150 in their pocket. Holding out for that money means more weeks of showings and the risk of losing the deal. For the agent, a quick sale is often the better deal. For you, it isn't.

Levitt compared homes owned by agents with homes they sold for clients. When agents sold **their own homes**, they kept them on the market **about ten days longer** and sold them for **a few percent more**. Same expertise, different incentive.

The chapter also looks at **listing language**. Vague, upbeat words like "fantastic" or "charming" tended to go with lower prices, while specific words about the house, like "granite" or "maple," went with higher ones. Agents know things buyers and sellers don't, and that knowledge can be used in the agent's interest.

## The Lesson
An expert's advice is shaped by **their** incentives. When their payoff is small and yours is large, they may push you to settle too early.

## When to Use This
- You're relying on a broker, agent, recruiter or consultant
- An advisor is pushing you to "take the offer now"
- You're designing a commission or fee structure

## Try This
- Work out exactly what your advisor gains from each option you're weighing.
- Ask, "What would you do if this were your own house, money or career?"
