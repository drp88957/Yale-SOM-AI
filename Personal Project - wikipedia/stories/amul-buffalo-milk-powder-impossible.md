# "You Can't Make Milk Powder from Buffalo Milk"

**One-line hook:** Global experts said it couldn't be done, and a small Indian cooperative did it anyway.

## The Story
India's dairy farmers, especially in Gujarat, mostly kept **buffaloes**, not cows. In the rainy season, milk was plentiful and farmers had surplus that could spoil. Turning it into **milk powder** would solve the problem. But foreign experts and dairy companies said buffalo milk could not be made into powder; the technology was built for cow milk.

Kurien did not accept that. His colleague **H.M. Dalaya** worked on the problem and found a way to make powder from buffalo milk. Amul built a plant to do it. This broke the belief that only cow milk countries could run a modern dairy industry, and it reduced India's reliance on foreign companies and imports.

Kurien saw the "expert" view as partly protecting the experts' own interests. Foreign firms had little reason to solve a problem that would help Indian farmers compete with them.

## The Lesson
"It can't be done" often means **"it hasn't been done by people like us"** or "it isn't in our interest to do." Test the claim yourself.

## When to Use This
- Experts tell you a local problem has no solution
- A big competitor's standard method doesn't fit your situation
- Your team accepts a limit nobody has tested
- You're building for a market that global players ignore

## Try This
- Write down the "impossible" assumption and ask: who says so, and do they benefit from it?
- Give a small, trusted team time and room to test the assumption directly.
