# Why Cows Thrive and Black Rhinos Vanish

**One-line hook:** Cows aren't endangered, but rhinos are. The difference is who owns them.

## The Story
Wheelan asks a simple question: why are some animals, like **cows and chickens**, in no danger of dying out, while the **black rhino** was being hunted toward extinction? Rhinos are valuable because their horns sell for high prices on illegal markets. So why doesn't that value protect them, the way it protects cows?

The answer is **ownership**. Cows belong to farmers, who have every reason to breed them, feed them and protect them. Wild rhinos belonged to no one in particular. For a poor local villager, a living rhino offered nothing, while a dead one could be worth a lot to a poacher. Bans and fences alone struggled to stop that.

Wheelan describes the approach some African countries took: giving **local communities a stake** in wildlife, for example through tourism and hunting income. When villagers earned money from living animals, they had a reason to **protect them** and to help stop poachers. Conservation worked better when it lined up with local incentives instead of fighting them.

## The Lesson
People protect what they **own or benefit from**. If you want something cared for, make sure someone gains from caring for it.

## When to Use This
- A shared resource (a codebase, office, budget) keeps getting neglected
- Rules alone aren't stopping bad behavior
- You want people to protect something long term

## Try This
- Give a specific person or team clear ownership, and let them share in the benefit.
- Ask, "Who gains if this thrives, and who gains if it's destroyed?"
