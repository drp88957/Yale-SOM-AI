# Alex Rogo's Trap: The Better He Does, the Faster His Companies Get Sold

**One-line hook:** Alex is told to fix three companies so the board can sell them, and doing his job well might cost his people their jobs.

## The Story
In the sequel to The Goal, Alex Rogo has been promoted to run a diversified group of three companies: a **cosmetics** company, a **steam equipment** maker (Pressure Steam), and a **printing** company. The board decides to sell the group to raise cash. Alex is caught in a painful conflict. If he improves the companies, they become more attractive and **sell faster**, and new owners may cut the managers and workers who trusted him. If he doesn't improve them, they'll be sold cheaply anyway, with the same risk.

Instead of picking the least-bad option, Alex uses Jonah's **thinking processes**. He maps the conflict as an **"evaporating cloud"**: both sides share a common goal, and each side rests on hidden assumptions. By questioning those assumptions, he looks for a third path. His aim becomes making the companies so strong and profitable that keeping them, or selling them on good terms that protect his people, becomes the better deal.

## The Lesson
**Most "either/or" dilemmas rest on an assumption you haven't questioned.** Write down both sides and what each one needs, and the way out often shows up.

## When to Use This
- You're stuck choosing between two bad options
- Your boss's goal seems to conflict with your team's interests
- A negotiation feels like pure win-lose
- You're doing well but worry it'll backfire on you

## Try This
- Draw the conflict: the shared goal, the two needs, the two opposing actions. Then list the assumptions behind each arrow.
- Pick the weakest assumption and ask: "What would have to be true for this to be false?"
