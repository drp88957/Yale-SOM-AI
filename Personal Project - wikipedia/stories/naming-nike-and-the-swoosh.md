# "Dimension Six" vs. "Nike": A Brand Born Under Deadline

**One-line hook:** The world's most famous sports brand got its name in a rush, and its founder wasn't sure he liked it.

## The Story
When Phil Knight's company needed its own brand, separate from the Japanese Tiger shoes it had been selling, the team had to pick a name **fast**, because the factory needed it for the shoes and boxes. Knight's own favorite was **"Dimension Six."** His colleagues didn't like it.

Knight's first employee, **Jeff Johnson**, came up with **"Nike,"** the Greek goddess of victory, saying the idea came to him in a dream. Knight wasn't convinced, but with the deadline here, he went with it, saying something like "maybe it'll grow on us."

The logo had a similar story. Knight hired a design student, **Carolyn Davidson**, for a small fee to design a mark. She produced what became the **Swoosh**. Knight's reaction was lukewarm: he didn't love it, but hoped it would grow on him. Both the name and the logo became among the most valuable brand assets in the world.

## The Lesson
**Don't let perfection stall a launch.** A good-enough choice made on time can become great through what you build behind it, and the founder's taste isn't always right.

## When to Use This
- You're stuck debating a name, logo, or detail
- You're the boss and your favorite option keeps losing
- A deadline forces a decision before you feel sure
- You're overvaluing first impressions of a new idea

## Try This
- Set a decision deadline for small but sticky choices, and stick to it.
- When your team prefers an option you don't love, ask: "Is my objection about the work or my ego?"
