# Thiel's Warning: Don't Mess Up the Culture

**One-line hook:** When Peter Thiel invested in Airbnb, Brian Chesky asked for his best advice. Thiel told him five words, and they became a company creed.

## The Story
As Airbnb grew, it kept raising money from bigger and more famous investors. One of them was **Founders Fund**, the venture firm co-founded by **Peter Thiel**, who had co-founded PayPal and was an early investor in Facebook. When the deal was done, Chesky did what he often did with powerful people. He asked a direct question. What was the single most important piece of advice Thiel could give him?

Gallagher reports Thiel's answer: **"Don't f\*\*\* up the culture."**

Chesky was surprised. He had just raised a huge amount of money. He expected advice about strategy, growth or hiring executives. Instead, Thiel told him that the culture was the thing most likely to break as Airbnb got bigger. Thiel had seen it happen at other companies. Once a company passes a certain size, he explained, culture starts to slip, and once it is gone it is almost impossible to get back.

Chesky took this to heart. He wrote a memo to the whole company about why culture mattered. Gallagher summarizes his argument. Culture, he wrote, is a shared way of doing things with passion. When the culture is strong, you can trust people to do the right thing without needing a thick rulebook. The stronger the culture, the less process a company needs. And a strong culture lets a company act like a family business even as it grows huge. He wanted every employee to feel responsible for protecting it.

Airbnb then built that belief into how it worked. The clearest example was **hiring**. Chesky believed every new hire either strengthened the culture or weakened it. So Airbnb added a separate **core values interview** to its hiring process. These interviews were run by employees from outside the team the candidate would join. Their only job was to judge whether the person fit the company's values, not whether they had the right skills. A candidate could be brilliant at the job and still be turned down if they failed the values test.

Gallagher describes how slowly Chesky hired in the early days. He personally interviewed many of the first employees and was known for taking months to fill a role. He would ask unusual questions to get at what candidates really cared about. He wanted people who would be excited about Airbnb's mission, not just about working at a hot startup.

The company's **core values** spelled out what it was looking for. One of the early values was **"Be a cereal entrepreneur."** It honored the Obama O's story and asked employees to be creative and scrappy when resources were tight. Others asked people to champion the mission, to be hosts and to embrace adventure. Over time, the list was revised, but the idea stayed the same: the values were meant to guide decisions when no one was watching.

The point of all this effort was scale. In a small startup, everyone knows the founders and absorbs the culture by being in the same room. In a company of thousands spread across many countries, that no longer works. Values, hiring and shared stories have to do the job instead.

Culture also showed up in the office. Gallagher describes Airbnb's San Francisco headquarters, where many conference rooms were built as copies of real Airbnb listings around the world. Walking into a meeting could feel like walking into a host's home in another country. The goal was to keep the hosts and guests in front of employees every day. The company also created a team focused on the **employee experience**, treating employees with the same care it asked hosts to show their guests.

In 2014, the culture work reached the brand itself. Airbnb rebranded around a new mission: **"Belong Anywhere."** The company said its real product was not a cheap room. It was the feeling of being at home anywhere in the world. It introduced a new logo called the **"Bélo,"** meant to be a simple symbol of belonging that anyone could draw. The internet mocked it right away, with many people saying it looked like body parts. Chesky did not back down. To him, "belonging" was the story that tied together hosts, guests and employees.

Gallagher does not pretend the culture was perfect. Airbnb's intense pace burned some people out, and Chesky could be demanding. But she shows a company that took Thiel's warning seriously and treated culture as something to design, protect and talk about openly, just like a product.

## Details Worth Remembering
- Thiel's advice after Founders Fund invested: "Don't f\*\*\* up the culture."
- Chesky turned it into a memo to all employees about why culture matters.
- His argument: a strong culture means you need fewer rules and processes.
- Airbnb added separate core values interviews, run by people outside the hiring team.
- Chesky hired very slowly early on and interviewed many early employees himself.
- An early core value was "Be a cereal entrepreneur."
- Many HQ meeting rooms copied real listings.
- The 2014 "Belong Anywhere" rebrand introduced the Bélo logo, which was widely mocked.

## The Lesson
**Culture is easiest to protect before you need it.** As a group grows, it can't run on the founders' daily presence, so it needs shared values, careful hiring and stories everyone knows. Hire for values as seriously as for skills, because every new person either strengthens the culture or waters it down.

## When to Use This
- Your team is about to grow quickly
- You're hiring and tempted to take a skilled person who doesn't fit
- People are asking for more rules because trust is slipping
- You're writing or revising team values
- You've just raised money or won a big win and feel invincible

## Try This
- Write down three values you'd turn down a talented candidate for breaking.
- Add one interview to your hiring process that only tests values, run by someone outside the team.
- Find a story from your team's history (like the cereal) and turn it into a value people remember.
- Notice where you're adding rules. Ask if a clearer culture would make the rule unnecessary.
