# Your Busiest Product Line Might Be Losing You Money

**One-line hook:** When Koch's consulting teams broke profits down by customer and product, companies often found that a small slice paid for everything, and some of the rest destroyed value.

## The Story
Before writing the book, Richard Koch worked as a **strategy consultant**. He describes a pattern he and his colleagues saw again and again when they analyzed client businesses: when you look at **profit** (not just revenue) for each product and each customer, the results are **wildly uneven**.

Typically, a **small minority of products and customers produced most of the profit**. Many others barely broke even, and some actually **lost money** once you counted the real costs of serving them: special handling, discounts, extra sales effort, complexity. Managers were often surprised, because the company's accounts showed only averages, which hid this imbalance. Busy, high-revenue lines sometimes turned out to be among the worst.

Koch's advice is to **analyze your business at a fine level**, find the profitable core, and then **put more effort into the vital few** while simplifying, repricing, or dropping the unprofitable many. He argues that **simplicity is profitable** and complexity quietly drains value.

## The Lesson
Averages hide the truth. **Measure profit by customer and product**, and you'll usually find a small set worth doubling down on, and a tail that's costing you.

## When to Use This
- Revenue is growing but profit isn't
- You're deciding which clients or products to keep
- Your team is stretched thin serving every customer the same way
- You're setting prices and treating all customers alike

## Try This
- Rank your customers (or projects) by real profit after all costs, not by revenue. Look at the top 20 percent and the bottom 20 percent.
- For the bottom group, choose one action: raise prices, serve them more simply, or let them go.
