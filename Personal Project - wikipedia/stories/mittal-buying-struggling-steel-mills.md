# Mittal's Contrarian Bet: Buying the Steel Mills Nobody Wanted

**One-line hook:** While others fled a dying industry, Lakshmi Mittal built a global empire by buying failing steel plants cheaply and fixing them.

## The Story
**Lakshmi Mittal** came from a family steel business in India. Rather than stay within India's limits on growth, he went abroad, starting with a mill in **Indonesia**. His real strategy took shape over the following decades: buy **struggling, often state-owned steel plants** in places other companies avoided, usually at low prices, then turn them around.

Cold Steel describes how this approach took Mittal to countries across the world, including the Caribbean, Latin America, and the former Soviet bloc. Where others saw losses and old equipment, Mittal saw **assets priced below their real value**. His teams brought in tighter management, cost controls, and know-how from plants they'd already fixed, and they repeated the playbook.

By the mid-2000s, this steady, unglamorous approach had made Mittal Steel one of the world's largest steelmakers and gave Mittal the scale to bid for Arcelor.

## The Lesson
**The best opportunities are often in places others have written off.** A repeatable turnaround playbook can turn many small bets into something huge.

## When to Use This
- You're looking at an "unattractive" market or role others avoid
- You're building a strategy based on doing one thing well, over and over
- You're an outsider trying to break into an established industry
- You're thinking about Indian companies going global

## Try This
- List three things in your field everyone thinks are "dead." Ask what it would take to make one work.
- Write down your own repeatable playbook: the steps you used last time something succeeded.
