# Young Rockefeller Keeps His Head in the Panic

**One-line hook:** While others panicked in a financial crisis, a teenage bookkeeper was learning the habit that would make him the richest man in America.

## The Story
Ryan Holiday opens with **John D. Rockefeller** as a young man working as a bookkeeper in Cleveland when the **Panic of 1857** hit. The economy collapsed, and many people around him panicked, lost fortunes, or froze.

Rockefeller did not. He treated the crisis as a **lesson**. He watched closely, learned how markets and businesses behaved under stress, and built a habit of **staying calm and unemotional** when others weren't. He saved money, kept his judgment clear, and stayed alert for opportunities.

Holiday argues this habit shaped his whole career. In later crises, when others were selling in fear, Rockefeller could see clearly and act, often buying when prices were low. His **composure under pressure** became one of his greatest strengths.

## The Lesson
Crises reward people who can **control their emotions and keep seeing clearly**. The obstacle is also a classroom.

## When to Use This
- Markets, your company or your job are in turmoil
- Everyone around you is panicking
- You're about to make a big decision while upset
- You're early in your career and facing a hard time

## Try This
- When something bad happens, write down: "What is actually true right now? What can I learn from this?"
- Make a rule: no major decisions until you've slept on it.
