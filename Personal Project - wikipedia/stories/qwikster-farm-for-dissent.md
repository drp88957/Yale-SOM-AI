# Qwikster: The Mistake Everyone Saw but Nobody Stopped

**One-line hook:** Netflix's CEO pushed through a plan that angered customers, and later found out many employees had doubted it all along.

## The Story
In 2011, Reed Hastings decided to split Netflix in two: streaming would stay as Netflix, and the DVD-by-mail business would move to a new company called **Qwikster**. Customers would need two accounts and would pay more overall. The reaction was furious. Netflix lost many subscribers, its stock fell sharply, and the company soon dropped the Qwikster plan.

Afterward, Hastings learned that **many people inside Netflix had serious doubts** but didn't push hard against him. His confidence and track record made people hold back. Even in a culture that valued candor, disagreement with the CEO came out softly or not at all.

The fix Netflix adopted was to **"farm for dissent"**: before a big decision, the person proposing it actively seeks out opposing views, for example by sharing a memo where people can comment and rate the idea. It's not enough to allow disagreement; a leader has to go out and **collect it**.

## The Lesson
**Silence is not agreement.** The more powerful you are, the harder you have to work to hear the "no."

## When to Use This
- You're about to make a big, hard-to-reverse decision
- Nobody in meetings ever disagrees with you
- You notice people saying "it's interesting" instead of "I think it's wrong"
- You're senior and new people defer to you

## Try This
- Share your next major proposal as a short doc and ask each reader to rate it and write their strongest objection.
- Privately ask the most likely skeptic: "What would make this fail?"
