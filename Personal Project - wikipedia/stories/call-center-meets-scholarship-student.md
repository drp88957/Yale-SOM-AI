# Five Minutes With a Scholarship Student

**One-line hook:** Fundraising callers who briefly met one student their work helped went on to raise far more money.

## The Story
Adam Grant studied a university call center where students phoned alumni to ask for donations. The job was dull and discouraging: most calls ended in rejection, and turnover was high. Pep talks and incentives weren't fixing it.

Grant ran an experiment. One group of callers spent **a few minutes meeting a student whose scholarship had been funded** by the money they raised. The student explained how the scholarship had changed his life. A comparison group didn't have this meeting.

In the following weeks, the callers who met the scholarship student spent **much more time on the phone and brought in dramatically more money**, a multiple of what they had raised before. Nothing else about the job had changed. What changed was that they could now **see the person their work helped**. Grant uses this to show that givers stay motivated, and avoid burnout, when they see the impact of their giving.

## The Lesson
**Seeing the impact of your work on a real person is one of the strongest motivators there is.** Meaning beats pep talks.

## When to Use This
- Your team does repetitive work and feels disconnected from results
- People seem burned out even though the workload is normal
- You're designing onboarding or team rituals
- You yourself have lost track of why your work matters

## Try This
- Invite one real customer or user to tell your team, in their own words, how your work affected them.
- Share one short "impact story" at the start of a weekly meeting.
