# When the Factory Is Fixed, the Constraint Moves to the Market

**One-line hook:** Alex's plants can now make far more than they sell, so the bottleneck is no longer inside the building.

## The Story
After the turnaround in The Goal, Alex Rogo's plants have **spare capacity**. The constraint is now the **market**: there aren't enough orders. Cutting prices would only start a price war. So Alex's team uses Jonah's tools to dig into what is really hurting their customers, not just what customers ask for.

The idea that runs through the book is to build an **offer the customer can't refuse**, one that solves a real problem for the customer and that competitors can't easily copy. Because Alex's plants are now fast and reliable, they can promise things others can't, such as much **quicker and more dependable delivery**. Customers will pay for that, because waiting or running out costs them far more than the price difference. The team also learns to **segment the market**, so it can sell its extra capacity in new segments without dragging down prices for existing customers.

## The Lesson
**Once you fix your operations, use those strengths to fix the customer's problem.** Don't compete on price when you can compete on something they value more.

## When to Use This
- Sales are flat and the first idea on the table is a discount
- Your team has gotten much better at something customers don't know about yet
- You're pitching a product against a bigger competitor
- You have spare capacity and are tempted to dump it cheaply

## Try This
- Ask a customer: "What does it cost you when things go wrong with suppliers like us?" Build your offer around that pain.
- Write down a promise only you can keep, then price it on the value to the customer, not your cost.
