# Grant at Vicksburg: Try Everything Until Something Works

**One-line hook:** Grant failed again and again to take a fortress city, until one bold idea finally worked.

## The Story
During the American Civil War, **Ulysses S. Grant** needed to capture **Vicksburg**, a fortified city on the Mississippi River. Its position on high bluffs, with swamps and rivers around it, made it extremely hard to attack.

Holiday describes how Grant tried **one approach after another**. His army tried digging canals and finding routes through the waterways and swamps. Most attempts failed. Critics complained, and the press attacked him. But Grant kept experimenting rather than giving up or repeating the same failed move.

Finally he made a daring choice: sending gunboats **past the city's guns at night**, marching his army down the other side of the river, crossing, and attacking from an unexpected direction. After a siege, Vicksburg surrendered. Holiday uses this to show **persistence plus creativity**: if one path is blocked, try another, and another.

## The Lesson
**Persistence isn't repeating the same thing harder.** It's trying different approaches until one works, while ignoring the noise of critics.

## When to Use This
- You've tried a few things and nothing has worked
- People are criticizing your slow progress
- A problem seems blocked from every direction
- Deciding whether to give up or try a new approach

## Try This
- List every possible path to the goal, even weird ones. Try the next one, not the same one again.
- Separate "the goal" from "the method." Keep the goal; change the method.
