# The Boat Crew Swap: Same Team, New Leader, Different Results

**One-line hook:** Instructors swapped the leaders of the best and worst boat crews, and the worst crew started winning.

## The Story
During SEAL training (BUD/S), trainees race in boat crews of about seven, carrying heavy rubber boats over the beach and paddling through surf. Winning crews get to rest; losing crews get punished. One crew, **Boat Crew II**, kept finishing last. Its leader blamed his men and said he had been given a bad team. **Boat Crew VI** kept winning.

The instructors tried an experiment: they **swapped only the two leaders**. Everyone else stayed. The leader from the winning crew took over Boat Crew II and focused it on a clear goal and working together. Boat Crew II began winning races. Boat Crew VI, which had already built good habits under its old leader, still performed well. The men in Boat Crew II hadn't changed; the leadership had.

## The Lesson
**There are no bad teams, only bad leaders.** The leader sets the standard. What a leader tolerates becomes the team's real standard.

## When to Use This
- You're convinced your team is "just not good enough"
- You inherit an underperforming team or department
- You're deciding whether to replace people or change how they're led
- A team has accepted losing as normal

## Try This
- Before blaming the team, write down the standard you have actually been enforcing (not the one you say you want).
- Pick one clear, shared goal for the next two weeks and point every discussion at it.
