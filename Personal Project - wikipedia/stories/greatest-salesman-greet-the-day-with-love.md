# "I Will Greet This Day with Love in My Heart": The Scroll That Opens Hearts

**One-line hook:** The second scroll's claim is that force can split a shield but only love can open a person's heart, so a salesman who cannot care about his customers will never really sell.

## The Story
After thirty days with the first scroll, Hafid has his method: three readings a day, the last one aloud, until a principle becomes a habit. Now he unrolls the scroll marked II, and it gives him the first thing to build that habit around. Its opening line is the vow he will repeat for the next month: **"I will greet this day with love in my heart."**

The scroll does not treat love as a soft extra. It calls it **the greatest secret of success in all ventures**. Its argument is practical. Strength of body can split a shield and even destroy life, but only the unseen power of love can **open the hearts of men**. A salesman can be clever, well dressed and full of arguments, but if the customer senses contempt or indifference, every door stays shut. Until the reader masters this art, the scroll says, they will be no more than a peddler in the marketplace.

So how does a person learn to love on command? The scroll's answer is to change how they see everything, starting with the world around them. The reader will **love the sun** because it warms their bones, and **love the rain** because it cleanses their spirit. They will love the light because it shows the way, and **love the darkness because it shows the stars**. They will welcome happiness because it enlarges the heart, and endure sadness because it opens the soul. They will accept rewards because they are earned, and welcome obstacles because they are a challenge. Every pair takes something pleasant and something unpleasant and finds a reason to embrace both.

Then the scroll turns to people, which is where a salesman's fortune is made or lost. The reader will **praise their enemies, and they will become friends**. They will encourage their friends, and they will become like brothers. They will look for reasons to applaud rather than excuses to gossip. When tempted to criticise, they will bite their tongue. When moved to praise, they will say it out loud. The scroll's point is that people respond to how they are treated, and that a habit of appreciation changes the people around you as much as it changes you.

The most memorable instruction is a private one. Whenever the reader meets another person, they will **silently say to themselves "I love you."** The words are never spoken out loud. But the scroll insists they will show anyway: in the reader's eyes, in the warmth of their voice, in the way they listen. The other person will feel it without knowing why, and their heart will open. This applies to everyone, the scroll says: to the great and the humble, to the rich and the poor, to those who buy and to those who don't.

The scroll also asks the reader to love types of people they would normally avoid. They will love the ambitious, because they can inspire, and the failures, because they can teach. They will love the kings because they are only human, the meek because they are divine, the rich because they are lonely, and the poor because there are so many of them. Each category gets a reason to be understood rather than resented.

Finally, the scroll turns the love inward. The reader must also love **themselves**. They will care for their body by keeping it clean and moderate. They will feed their mind with wisdom rather than idle thoughts, and they will tend their soul rather than letting it grow complacent. Only someone who respects themselves can offer real warmth to others.

It also links back to how the story began. Hafid did not earn the scrolls by selling. He earned them by wrapping a stranger's baby in a robe on a cold night, an act of pure care with nothing to gain. The second scroll asks him to turn that one moment of feeling into a daily discipline, offered to every person he meets.

For a camel boy who began his career by being mocked in the streets of Bethlehem, this scroll is a quiet revolution. The salesman's enemy, it suggests, is not the stubborn customer but his own cold heart. Hafid reads it three times a day for a month. By the end, greeting each day and each person with love is no longer an effort. It is simply how he walks into the market.

## Details Worth Remembering
- Opening vow: "I will greet this day with love in my heart."
- Love is called the greatest secret of success in all ventures.
- Muscle can split a shield, but only love can open the hearts of men.
- Pairs of opposites: love the sun and the rain, the light and the darkness that shows the stars.
- Praise your enemies and they become friends; encourage your friends and they become brothers.
- Silently say "I love you" to every person you meet, and it will show in your eyes and voice.
- The scroll ends by asking the reader to care for their own body, mind and soul.

## The Lesson
**People can feel whether you care about them, and that feeling decides whether they open up.** Technique without goodwill reads as manipulation. Genuine warmth, practised as a daily habit, does more for persuasion than any clever argument.

## When to Use This
- You're walking into a tough sales meeting or negotiation
- You have a difficult client, colleague or rival you've started to resent
- Your pitch is technically strong but people still aren't buying
- You notice you've slipped into gossip or criticism at work
- You're trying to build long-term relationships, not just close deals

## Try This
- Before your next meeting, silently tell yourself you wish the other person well, and notice whether your tone changes.
- Pick one person you dislike and find one honest thing to praise about them this week.
- When you feel the urge to criticise someone, wait a day and see if you still need to.
