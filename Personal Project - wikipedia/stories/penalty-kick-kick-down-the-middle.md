# Why Penalty Takers Don't Shoot Down the Middle

**One-line hook:** In soccer penalty kicks, aiming straight at the goalkeeper works well, so why do so few players do it?

## The Story
In a penalty kick, the kicker and goalkeeper are only a few meters apart. The ball moves too fast for the keeper to react, so the keeper must **guess and dive** left or right at the moment of the kick. Data show that keepers **very rarely stay in the center**.

That means a kick aimed **straight down the middle** succeeds more often than one aimed at a corner. Yet kickers rarely choose it. Why? Levitt and Dubner point to **private incentives**. If you shoot to a corner and the keeper guesses right, people say the keeper made a great save. If you shoot down the middle and the keeper just stands there and catches it, you look like a **fool** in front of millions of fans.

So the kicker's choice is shaped by the fear of **looking stupid** as much as by the goal of scoring. The team's interest (more goals) and the player's interest (protecting his reputation) point in different directions.

## The Lesson
People often pick the option that **protects their reputation** over the one most likely to succeed. The best move can be the unconventional one that looks bad if it fails.

## When to Use This
- You're choosing between a "safe-looking" option and one with better odds
- Your team avoids bold ideas because of how failure would look
- You're designing rewards and want people to take smart risks

## Try This
- Separate "Which choice has the best odds?" from "Which choice is easiest to defend?"
- Tell your team ahead of time that a smart, unusual bet that fails won't be punished.
