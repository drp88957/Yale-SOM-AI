# Leading Up: When Headquarters "Just Doesn't Get It"

**One-line hook:** Frustrated by headquarters' endless questions and approvals, the SEAL leaders realized the real problem was them.

## The Story
In Ramadi, the SEAL task unit needed approval from higher headquarters before missions, and headquarters kept asking for more detail and more paperwork. To the people on the ground, it felt like the bosses were **out of touch and slowing them down**. Complaints about "them" became common.

Jocko's view was that blaming the boss is just another excuse. Headquarters didn't have the picture the SEALs had. So the job of the people on the ground was to **give their leaders the information they needed** to say yes: clear plans, regular updates, and context about what was happening. When the team owned that communication, approvals came faster and the relationship improved. The same applies downward: leaders must explain the bigger picture to their own teams, who also can't see what the leader sees.

## The Lesson
**If your boss isn't making the decisions you need, look first at what you aren't giving them.** Leading up is your responsibility, not theirs.

## When to Use This
- Your manager or client keeps asking for "more detail" or blocking approvals
- You hear your team say "leadership doesn't understand"
- You're stuck waiting on a decision from someone above you
- You're the boss and your team seems confused about priorities

## Try This
- Send your boss a short, regular update with the situation, your plan, and what you need from them.
- Ask your manager: "What would you need to see to feel comfortable approving this?"
