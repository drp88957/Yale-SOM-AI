# The CIA Analyst Who Spoke Up Too Early, Then Won

**One-line hook:** Carmen Medina pushed the CIA to share intelligence online, got sidelined for it, and later succeeded by changing how she spoke up.

## The Story
Grant tells the story of **Carmen Medina**, a CIA analyst who believed the agency's way of sharing intelligence was badly outdated. Information moved through slow, printed reports. She argued that analysts should share intelligence **online, in real time**, more like the internet.

Her early efforts backfired. She pushed her idea hard before she had the **status and credibility** to make the case, and the reaction from leaders was negative. Grant describes how her career suffered and she was moved aside for a time. Later, after earning more standing in the organization, she raised the ideas again, this time **with more support and in a way that fit the organization's goals**. This time it worked, and the CIA moved toward online sharing tools, including a wiki-style platform for intelligence analysts.

Grant uses Medina's story to show that **speaking up is a skill**. Status matters: people with established credibility have more freedom to challenge norms. He also stresses framing new ideas in terms of **values others already hold**, and finding allies, rather than just declaring the current system broken.

## The Lesson
Being right isn't enough. **Earn credibility, find allies, and frame your idea in terms others care about** before you push hard against the status quo.

## When to Use This
- You see a big problem at work but you're junior
- Your past attempts to push change were shut down
- You want your team members to challenge ideas more effectively
- You're trying to change a slow, traditional organization

## Try This
- Before your next big proposal, list two respected people who might support it, and talk with them first.
- Rewrite your pitch to connect the change to a goal your boss already cares about.
