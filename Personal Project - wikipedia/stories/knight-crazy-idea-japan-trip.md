# The "Crazy Idea": Phil Knight Walks into a Japanese Shoe Company

**One-line hook:** A young graduate with no company and no product invented a business name on the spot to win a meeting in Japan.

## The Story
At Stanford business school, Phil Knight wrote a paper arguing that **Japanese running shoes** could do to the American market what Japanese cameras had done: offer quality at a lower price and take on the German leaders. He called it his **"Crazy Idea."**

In 1962, at 24, he set off on a trip around the world. In Japan, he went to Kobe and talked his way into a meeting with **Onitsuka**, the maker of **Tiger** running shoes. When the executives asked what company he represented, Knight had none. He made one up on the spot: **Blue Ribbon Sports**. He told them he wanted to distribute Tigers in the United States. The executives agreed to send samples.

When the shoes arrived, Knight sent a pair to his old track coach at the University of Oregon, **Bill Bowerman**, hoping for an order. Instead Bowerman offered to become his **partner**. They each put in a small amount of money, and Knight started selling shoes out of the **trunk of his car** at track meets. That business became Nike.

## The Lesson
**Sometimes you have to act before you feel ready.** Showing up with confidence, and a clear idea of what you want, can open doors that credentials never would.

## When to Use This
- You have an idea but no title, team, or resources yet
- You're about to talk yourself out of a bold meeting or ask
- You're starting a side project and waiting for "the right time"
- You need a first partner or mentor

## Try This
- Write your own "Crazy Idea" in one paragraph: the gap you see and why now.
- Send one bold ask this week to someone who could help, and show up as if the business already exists.
