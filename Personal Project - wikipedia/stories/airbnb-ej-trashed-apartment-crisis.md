# The Trashed Apartment: Airbnb's First Big Trust Crisis

**One-line hook:** One host's ransacked home nearly broke Airbnb, and the fix started with the CEO admitting "we screwed up."

## The Story
In 2011, a San Francisco host who went by **"EJ"** wrote a blog post describing how a guest had **ransacked her apartment**, stealing belongings and destroying her home. The story spread quickly. Airbnb's first response was slow and seemed more focused on protecting the company than on helping her, which made things much worse.

As the backlash grew, **Brian Chesky** realized the company's whole model, strangers trusting strangers, was at stake. He wrote a public letter **admitting the company had handled it badly**, owning the mistake directly instead of hedging.

Airbnb then made real changes. It announced a **guarantee to protect hosts** against property damage (initially $50,000, later raised), set up a **24-hour customer support** line, and improved safety and verification features. The crisis became a turning point in how seriously Airbnb treated trust and safety.

## The Lesson
**When trust breaks, a fast, honest apology plus a concrete fix matters more than defending yourself.** A crisis handled well can make you stronger.

## When to Use This
- You or your team made a public mistake
- Your first response to a complaint was defensive
- You run something that depends on strangers trusting each other
- You need to rebuild credibility after a failure

## Try This
- When you mess up, say clearly what went wrong and what you'll change, in that order, without excuses.
- Ask: "What guarantee could I offer that would make this risk feel safe for others?"
