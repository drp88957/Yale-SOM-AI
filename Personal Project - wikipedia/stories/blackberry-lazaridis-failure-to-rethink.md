# The BlackBerry Inventor Who Couldn't Rethink His Own Invention

**One-line hook:** The genius who created the smartphone people couldn't put down lost it all by refusing to question what made it great.

## The Story
**Mike Lazaridis** co-founded Research in Motion and created the **BlackBerry**, the device that made email on your phone a must-have for business people. He was a brilliant engineer, and for years the BlackBerry dominated.

Then the **iPhone** arrived. Lazaridis was confident that customers wanted a physical keyboard, security, and battery life, not a phone built for apps and entertainment. When his own team pushed to rethink the device, he resisted. He stayed attached to the design that had made BlackBerry successful. As people moved to touchscreen smartphones, BlackBerry's market share collapsed.

Grant's point is not that Lazaridis wasn't smart. It's that **intelligence can make rethinking harder**. Smart, successful people are good at defending their beliefs, and past success makes them feel even more right. Lazaridis was in **preacher mode** about his own invention, protecting it instead of testing whether it still fit the world.

## The Lesson
Your biggest success can become your biggest blind spot. The more an idea made you who you are, the more carefully you should question it.

## When to Use This
- A new competitor is changing what customers expect
- Your team suggests changing something you built or championed
- You find yourself saying "customers will always want X"
- Your past success is your main argument for the current plan

## Try This
- Ask your sharpest critic to make the strongest case that your core product is becoming outdated.
- Write down what would have to be true for your strategy to be wrong, and check those signals regularly.
