# Why Drug Dealers Still Live with Their Moms

**One-line hook:** A crack gang's real account books showed that most of its dealers earned less than minimum wage.

## The Story
Sociologist **Sudhir Venkatesh**, as a graduate student, spent years with a Chicago crack-selling gang. He ended up with something almost nobody had: the gang's **financial records**. Levitt and Venkatesh analyzed them.

The gang was organized much like a franchise business. A small group of top leaders made very good money, and a local leader did well. But the **foot soldiers** working the street corners earned **less than minimum wage**, while facing a real risk of arrest, injury and death. Many lived at home with their mothers because they couldn't afford anything else.

So why do it? Because the gang worked like a **tournament**. Like an aspiring actor waiting tables or a young lawyer chasing partnership, the foot soldiers were competing for a very small number of big prizes at the top. The success of the few hid the poverty of the many.

## The Lesson
In "winner-take-most" fields, the visible winners make the job look far better than it is for **the typical person** doing it.

## When to Use This
- You're judging a career path by its most famous success stories
- You're designing pay where a few top spots motivate a large base
- Someone is sacrificing for years on a long-shot promotion

## Try This
- Ask for the **typical** outcome, not the best case, before committing to a path.
- Estimate your real odds of reaching the "prize" level, and what you'll have if you don't.
