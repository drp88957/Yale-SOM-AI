# The Interview Question Almost Nobody Answers Well

**One-line hook:** Peter Thiel asks job candidates one question, and most people fail it by repeating what everyone already believes.

## The Story
Thiel opens Zero to One with the question he likes to ask in interviews: **"What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"**

It sounds easy, but it is hard. A good answer has to be *true*, *important*, and *unpopular* all at once. Most people answer with something that is already widely believed, or something that is unpopular because it is simply wrong. Thiel says the question is hard intellectually, because school teaches us accepted knowledge, and hard psychologically, because saying an unpopular truth takes courage.

Later in the book (Ch. 8, "Secrets"), Thiel builds on this. He argues that **great companies are built on secrets**: important things that are true but that most people have not noticed or do not believe yet. If you think there are no secrets left, you will never look for one, and you will only copy what already exists.

## The Lesson
Progress that goes from **"zero to one"** starts with a belief that is true but not yet popular. If your plan relies only on what everyone already agrees with, you are probably building something that already exists.

## When to Use This
- You're brainstorming a new product or business idea and everything sounds familiar
- You're interviewing a candidate and want to test independent thinking
- Your team agrees too fast on a strategy
- You're questioning a "best practice" everyone in your industry follows

## Try This
- Write down three answers to Thiel's question for your own field. Cross out any that a typical colleague would also say.
- In your next strategy meeting, ask: "What do we believe that our competitors don't?"
