# Airbnb's New York Photo Shoot: Doing Things That Don't Scale

**One-line hook:** Airbnb's growth jumped once the founders flew across the country and took nicer photos of hosts' apartments themselves.

## The Story
During Y Combinator, Airbnb's growth was flat. The founders looked closely at their listings in **New York**, one of their biggest markets, and noticed something simple: the **photos were bad**. Dark, blurry phone pictures made even good apartments look unappealing.

Paul Graham pushed them to go to their users in person, to do **"things that don't scale."** So the founders flew to New York, rented a good camera, and went door to door to **photograph hosts' homes** themselves. They also stayed with hosts and listened to their complaints and ideas about the product.

Revenue in New York rose soon after the better photos went up. The founders learned two lessons: quality photos built **trust** with guests, and meeting users face to face showed them problems they couldn't see in their data. Airbnb later turned free professional photography for hosts into a real program.

## The Lesson
**Early on, do the manual, unscalable work to deeply understand and delight your users.** The data can show a problem, but you often need to go see it to fix it.

## When to Use This
- Growth or engagement has stalled and you don't know why
- You're early in a project with only a few users
- The team is trying to automate before it understands the problem
- Trust is the main barrier for your customers

## Try This
- Personally help five users this week, start to finish, and note every point where they struggle.
- Look at your product's "first impression" (photos, landing page, first email) through a stranger's eyes.
