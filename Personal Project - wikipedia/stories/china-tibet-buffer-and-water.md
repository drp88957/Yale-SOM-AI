# Why China Will Never Let Go of Tibet

**One-line hook:** To Marshall, Tibet is less about human rights debates and more about high ground and water.

## The Story
Marshall describes China as built around a **Han Chinese heartland** in the fertile river plains of the north and east. Around that core sit buffer regions: Tibet, Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia. Each one protects the heartland from outside threats.

**Tibet** matters most. It is the high plateau where major rivers of Asia begin, often called a "water tower." It also sits on the border with India. Marshall's argument is that if China did not control Tibet, India might, giving a rival the high ground overlooking China's heartland and influence over its water. That's why, in his view, China treats Tibet as non-negotiable no matter how much criticism it receives. He also notes that the **Himalayas**, the wall between China and India, are a big reason these two giant neighbours have clashed at the border but have not fought a full-scale war through the mountains.

## The Lesson
What looks like stubbornness often protects a **core vital interest**. Find the thing a player cannot afford to lose, and their "unreasonable" behaviour starts to make sense.

## When to Use This
- You're doing business in China or India and want to read the political temperature
- A partner refuses to budge on one point no matter what you offer
- You're mapping out which risks are negotiable and which are not
- You want to understand India–China border tensions in the news

## Try This
- In a negotiation, separate the other side's "heartland" (must protect) from their "buffers" (can trade). Only bargain over buffers.
- For any supply you depend on, trace it back to its source. Who controls the "headwaters"?
