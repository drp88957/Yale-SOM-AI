# Diapers.com vs. Amazon: How a Giant Squeezed a Rising Rival

**One-line hook:** A fast-growing startup sold diapers better than Amazon, until Amazon decided to win at any price.

## The Story
**Quidsi**, the company behind **Diapers.com**, became a big hit with young parents by shipping bulky baby products fast and reliably. Amazon saw it as a threat in an important category, since parents who buy diapers online are valuable long-term customers. Amazon approached Quidsi about an acquisition, and the founders weren't interested.

Brad Stone describes what came next. Amazon **cut its prices on diapers and baby products** sharply, and Quidsi saw Amazon's prices react when it changed its own. Amazon also launched a program with big discounts aimed at parents. Quidsi's growth slowed, and investors got nervous about funding a long price war against a company that could lose money for years.

Quidsi's founders ended up selling. Walmart also bid, but Amazon won and **bought Quidsi**. Stone's account shows how Amazon could use its size and patience as a weapon.

## The Lesson
**A giant with deep pockets can outlast you on price.** If you compete head-on with one, have a plan for when it decides to come after you, and know your walk-away options.

## When to Use This
- You're a startup in a market a giant cares about
- You're deciding whether to accept an acquisition offer
- A competitor is undercutting you on price
- You're thinking about how big companies keep or lose their edge

## Try This
- Write down what your business has that a giant can't copy just by cutting prices (relationships, speed, niche focus).
- Before any buyout talk, list your alternatives so you know your real bargaining position.
