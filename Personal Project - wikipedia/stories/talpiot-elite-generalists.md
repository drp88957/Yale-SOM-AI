# Talpiot: Israel's Program for Training Problem-Solvers Across Fields

**One-line hook:** Israel picks a small group of its brightest teenagers and trains them to connect science, technology, and real military problems.

## The Story
After the **1973 Yom Kippur War**, when Israel was caught off guard, the military looked for ways to keep a technology edge. One answer was **Talpiot**, an elite program that selects a small number of top high school students each year.

Senor and Singer describe how Talpiot cadets get an intense education in **physics, math, and computer science**, along with exposure to many parts of the military. Instead of becoming narrow specialists, they're trained to see problems across different units and **combine technical knowledge with real-world needs**. After the program, they serve for years in technical and research roles.

Many Talpiot graduates later went on to found or lead technology companies. The book uses Talpiot as an example of how Israel invests heavily in finding talent early, then pushes people to work **across boundaries** between fields.

## The Lesson
**Breadth plus depth creates powerful problem-solvers.** People trained to connect fields, not just master one, find solutions specialists miss.

## When to Use This
- You're designing a training or rotation program
- You're deciding whether to specialize or broaden your skills
- You're hiring for roles that need people to connect different teams
- You're thinking about how to nurture a gifted student

## Try This
- Spend time this month learning how a neighboring team or field solves problems, and look for one idea to borrow.
- Pair technical people with front-line users on a real problem for a week.
