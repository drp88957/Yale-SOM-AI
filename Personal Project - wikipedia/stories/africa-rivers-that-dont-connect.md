# Africa's Rivers and the Borders Drawn With a Ruler

**One-line hook:** Africa has some of the world's longest rivers, yet many of them are hard to use for trade, and its borders were often drawn by outsiders who never saw the land.

## The Story
In his chapter on Africa, Marshall explains why geography has made development harder there. Many of Africa's great rivers have **waterfalls and rapids** that block ships from moving goods far inland, unlike the Mississippi or the Rhine. Much of the coastline has few natural deep harbours. Large deserts and jungles made it difficult for regions to connect and trade with each other.

Then came the second problem: **colonial borders**. European powers divided the continent largely to suit their own interests, often with straight lines on a map. These borders frequently cut across ethnic groups or forced rival groups into one state. Marshall argues that many of the conflicts that followed independence trace back to these **artificial lines**, which ignored how people actually lived on the ground.

## The Lesson
Systems designed from a distance, without respect for the people and terrain they cover, **create problems that last for generations**. Structure should follow reality, not the other way around.

## When to Use This
- You're redrawing team boundaries or territories from headquarters
- A merger forces groups with different cultures into one unit
- A market looks big on paper but is hard to reach in practice
- You're designing a system for people whose daily reality you haven't seen

## Try This
- Before drawing any "border" (org chart, sales territory, product line), talk to the people who live on both sides of it.
- When sizing a market, ask not just "How big?" but "How easy is it to move things across it?"
