# We Ask Google What We Would Never Ask Anyone Else

**One-line hook:** Galloway argues that Google has become a kind of modern god: all-knowing, always there, and trusted with our most private questions.

## The Story
Galloway describes **Google** as filling a role that religion and trusted authorities once played. When people have a question, a worry, or a secret, they type it into Google, often things they would **never ask a friend, a doctor, or a priest**. Google listens without judgment and answers instantly.

He points out how much **trust** people place in Google's results. Most people assume the top results are the most accurate and rarely question them. Search results also feel neutral and objective compared with ads, which gives Google enormous influence over what people believe and buy. At the same time, every question we ask gives Google **data** about our hopes, fears, and intentions, which it uses to sell targeted advertising.

Galloway's point is that this combination of **trust, knowledge, and data** has made Google extraordinarily powerful and profitable, and that we should think carefully about how much influence one company has over what we know.

## The Lesson
**Whoever answers people's questions holds enormous power and data.** Be aware of who you're trusting with your questions, and what they gain from it.

## When to Use This
- You're making a decision based mostly on top search results
- You're thinking about privacy and what tech companies know about you
- You're building a product that people would trust with sensitive questions
- You're explaining to someone why "free" services aren't really free
