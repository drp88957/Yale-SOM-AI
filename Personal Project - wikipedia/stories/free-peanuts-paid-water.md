# Free Peanuts, Paid Water

**One-line hook:** Why would a bar give away salty snacks but make you pay for a glass of water?

## The Story
Many bars offer **free salty snacks** like peanuts or pretzels, yet charge for water or are slow to give it out. At first glance it seems backwards: peanuts cost more than tap water.

Frank's explanation is about what each item does to the bar's **main source of profit**: drinks. **Salty snacks make people thirsty**, so they order more beer and cocktails. Giving away peanuts is like advertising that pays for itself. **Water** does the opposite. It quenches thirst and replaces drinks that the bar earns money on. So a profit-minded bar gives away what **boosts** its core sales and discourages what **reduces** them.

The same logic shows up all over business: things that drive a company's real profit center are often given away free or cheap, while things that compete with it are priced high.

## The Lesson
To understand a price, look at **how the item affects the seller's main profit**, not just what it costs to provide.

## When to Use This
- You're deciding what to give away free and what to charge for
- You're puzzled by a company's strange "freebies"
- You're designing a bundle or loyalty offer

## Try This
- List your offerings and mark which ones drive your core revenue; consider making those cheaper or free.
- Spot what competes with your profit center and stop subsidizing it.
