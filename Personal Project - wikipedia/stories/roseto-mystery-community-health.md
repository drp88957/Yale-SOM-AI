# Roseto: The Town Where Hearts Didn't Fail

**One-line hook:** A small Pennsylvania town had almost no heart disease, and the reason wasn't diet, genes or exercise.

## The Story
Roseto, Pennsylvania, was settled by immigrants from the village of Roseto Valfortore in southern Italy. In the late 1950s, a physician named **Stewart Wolf** heard that local doctors rarely saw heart disease among Rosetans under 65. At a time when heart attacks were a leading killer of American men, this made no sense.

Wolf and his colleagues checked the obvious explanations one by one. **Diet?** Rosetans cooked with lard and ate plenty of fat. **Exercise?** Not unusual. **Genes?** Relatives from the same Italian region who lived elsewhere in America didn't share the advantage. **Location?** Neighboring towns in the same area had normal rates of heart disease.

The answer was **the community itself**. Rosetans visited each other on the street, cooked for one another, and lived in three-generation households. They shared a church and belonged to many civic clubs. Wealthy residents didn't flaunt their money. The town had built a **tight, egalitarian social structure** that protected people from the stresses of modern life. To understand health, Wolf had to look beyond the individual to the culture around them.

## The Lesson
When an outcome looks like it comes from individual choices, look at the **environment and relationships** around the person too. The real cause may be outside the usual suspects.

## When to Use This
- You're analyzing a result and every "obvious" variable comes up empty
- You're trying to explain why one team or office does far better than the rest
- You're building culture and wondering whether social ties really matter
- You're thinking about well-being, burnout or retention

## Try This
- When diagnosing a performance gap, list the **social and structural** factors as well as the individual ones.
- Invest in rituals that build real connection, such as shared meals and mentoring across generations.
