# Would You Send Your Child to Mars?

**One-line hook:** Haidt asks whether you'd let a tech company take your child to live on Mars without proper safety testing, then points out we already did something similar.

## The Story
Haidt opens with a thought experiment. Imagine a billionaire offers to take your child to live on **Mars**, where they'll grow up in an environment that will reshape their body and development in ways nobody has tested. No parent would agree. Yet, Haidt argues, that is roughly what happened to a generation of children in the early 2010s, when they moved much of their lives onto **smartphones and social media**.

Companies designed these products to capture attention and keep young users engaged, with **little research** on how they would affect developing brains. There were few meaningful age checks, and parents had little guidance. Haidt argues that, rather than protecting children online, society did the reverse: **overprotecting kids in the real world and under-protecting them in the virtual world**. He links this shift to the sharp rise in teen anxiety and depression that began in the early 2010s.

## The Lesson
New environments deserve the same caution as new medicines. **"Everyone's doing it" is not a safety test.**

## When to Use This
- You're deciding when to give a child a phone or social media account
- Your team is rolling out a new tool to young or vulnerable users
- A new technology is spreading faster than anyone has studied it
- You're explaining tech risks to parents or educators

## Try This
- Before adopting something new for kids (or users), ask: "What evidence do we have that this is safe, not just popular?"
- Write down the age or condition at which you'll say yes, so the decision isn't made by default.
