# "But Everyone Else Has One": Breaking a Collective Action Trap

**One-line hook:** No parent wants to be the only one whose kid has no phone, which is exactly why every kid ends up with one.

## The Story
Haidt points out that many parents worry about smartphones and social media but give in anyway. Why? Because of a **collective action problem**. If your child is the only one without a phone or Instagram, they may feel left out of where their friends talk and make plans. So each family makes the choice that seems best for their own child, and together they create an outcome almost nobody wants.

Haidt's answer is to change the **norms together**, not family by family. He proposes **four norms**: no smartphones before high school (basic phones are fine), no social media before age 16, **phone-free schools** (phones locked away for the whole school day), and far more independence and free play in the real world. When many parents in a class or school agree together, no child is left out, and the pressure flips.

## The Lesson
Some problems **can't be solved by individuals acting alone**. When everyone is trapped by "but everyone else does it," the fix is a shared agreement.

## When to Use This
- You're a parent facing "everyone else has one" pressure
- Your team works late every night because nobody wants to leave first
- A bad industry practice persists because no single company wants to stop
- You're trying to change a habit across a school, club or office

## Try This
- Instead of changing alone, find a few others who share your concern and agree on a rule together.
- Make the new norm visible (a signed pledge, a school policy) so people know they're not alone.
