# Hansell vs. LeMay: The Idealist Who Was Fired and the Pragmatist Who Replaced Him

**One-line hook:** One general stuck to his principles and got poor results; the other got results and paid a terrible moral price.

## The Story
**Haywood Hansell** was a true believer in the Bomber Mafia's dream: precision bombing of key targets from high altitude, sparing civilians. In late 1944 he commanded the B-29 bombers flying from the Mariana Islands against Japan. But conditions were brutal. Clouds covered targets, and over Japan the bombers ran into the **jet stream**, extremely fast high-altitude winds that threw their aim off. Raid after raid failed to destroy the targets.

Washington lost patience. In early 1945 Hansell was replaced by **Curtis LeMay**, a tough, practical commander focused on results. LeMay abandoned high-altitude precision bombing and switched to **low-level night raids with firebombs** on Japanese cities. Militarily, the change "worked." Gladwell treats this as a real dilemma: Hansell was loyal to an ideal and failed at the job; LeMay succeeded at the job by doing something horrific. Gladwell openly wrestles with which man was right.

## The Lesson
Principles and results can pull in opposite directions. **Be clear about which you're optimising for**, and honest about the cost of each choice.

## When to Use This
- Your values-based approach isn't producing results and pressure is mounting
- You're replacing a leader and choosing between an idealist and a pragmatist
- A shortcut would hit the target but feels wrong
- You're judging a past decision that "worked" at a high cost

## Try This
- Write down your non-negotiables before the pressure hits, so you're not deciding them in a crisis.
- When results fall short, ask whether the principle is wrong or only the method. Change the method first.
