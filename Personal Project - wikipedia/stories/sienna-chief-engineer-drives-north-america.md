# The Engineer Who Drove Across North America to Redesign a Minivan

**One-line hook:** Toyota's chief engineer didn't study market reports about American families; he drove the roads they drive.

## The Story
When Toyota was redesigning the **Sienna** minivan for North America, the chief engineer, **Yuji Yokoya**, had never lived there. Instead of relying on reports, he practiced **genchi genbutsu**: "go and see for yourself." He **drove a Sienna through every U.S. state, every Canadian province, and Mexico**.

Along the way he noticed things no survey would have told him. Roads and **crosswinds** in some regions called for better handling and stability. In some places the streets were narrow, so the van needed a **tighter turning radius**. Most important, he saw how American families actually used minivans: kids and long trips, with lots of eating and drinking in the car. That led to more **cup holders** and room inside. He realized that in a minivan, the children in the back seats matter as much as the driver.

The redesigned Sienna was a big improvement, because it was designed around how people really lived.

## The Lesson
**You can't understand a problem from a desk.** Go to where the work or the customer actually is and see it with your own eyes.

## When to Use This
- You're designing a product for users you've never met
- You're managing a team or market in a different country
- Reports say one thing but results say another
- You're about to make a decision based only on a summary

## Try This
- Before your next big decision, spend time where the problem happens: shadow a user, sit with the front line, walk the process.
- Write down three things you saw that weren't in any report.
