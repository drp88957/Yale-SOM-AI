# Chutzpah and Young Commanders: How Israel's Army Trains Entrepreneurs

**One-line hook:** In Israel's army, very young officers make real decisions with little supervision, and that habit carries over into startups.

## The Story
Senor and Singer argue that the **Israel Defense Forces** act like a giant training school for entrepreneurs. Because Israel is small and nearly everyone serves, the military gives **young soldiers and officers serious responsibility** early. Officers in their early twenties lead units and make important calls in the field, often without waiting for orders from above.

The IDF also has relatively **little hierarchy** compared to many armies. The authors describe a culture where soldiers can question or argue with commanders, where improvising is valued, and where **mistakes made while trying something** are treated as part of learning rather than career-ending. They link this to Israeli **chutzpah**: a boldness that, at work, shows up as students challenging professors and junior engineers challenging CEOs.

Military service also creates networks. Employers in Israel often ask what unit a candidate served in, because it signals what they've done and been trusted with, more than which university they attended.

## The Lesson
**Giving people real responsibility early, and permission to challenge and fail, builds leaders faster than any course.**

## When to Use This
- You're deciding how much to trust a new or junior team member
- Your team waits for permission instead of taking initiative
- You're hiring and wondering what really signals ability
- You want a culture where people push back

## Try This
- Give a junior person full ownership of a real decision this month, with clear goals but no micromanaging.
- In interviews, ask: "Tell me about a time you were trusted with more than your title suggested."
