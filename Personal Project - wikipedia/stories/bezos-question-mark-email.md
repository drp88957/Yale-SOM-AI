# The One-Character Email: Bezos's "?"

**One-line hook:** At Amazon, the scariest email you could get from the CEO was a forwarded customer complaint with a single question mark.

## The Story
Jeff Bezos kept a public email address that customers could write to, and he read many of the messages. When a complaint caught his attention, he would **forward it to the responsible manager with just one character: "?"**

Brad Stone describes how Amazon employees treated these emails like a ticking bomb. The manager and team were expected to **drop everything**, dig into what went wrong, find the root cause, and send back a full explanation, often within hours. The "?" said, without any words, "Why did this happen, and how will you make sure it doesn't happen again?"

It was harsh, and Stone doesn't hide that Amazon could be a tough place to work. But the practice showed that **one customer's bad experience could be a signal of a bigger problem**, and that leaders at the very top cared about it.

## The Lesson
**What the leader pays attention to becomes what the company pays attention to.** Treat a single customer complaint as a clue to a system problem, not a one-off.

## When to Use This
- You want your team to take customer feedback seriously
- Complaints get handled case by case and never fixed at the root
- You're building culture and want to show what really matters
- You're on the receiving end of a hard question and need to own it

## Try This
- Pick one customer complaint a week and trace it all the way back to its cause.
- When you get a sharp question from your boss, reply with the cause, the fix, and how you'll prevent it, not just an apology.
