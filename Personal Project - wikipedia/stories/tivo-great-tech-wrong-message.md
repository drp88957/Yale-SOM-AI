# TiVo Had the Best Product and Still Lost

**One-line hook:** TiVo invented a product people loved, then sold it by listing features, and it never broke into the mass market.

## The Story
When **TiVo** came out, it was a breakthrough: a device that let you pause live TV, skip commercials, and record shows easily. It had good funding, strong press, and a great product. By the usual logic, it should have dominated.

But Sinek says TiVo marketed itself by **what** it did: here's a box, it can pause live TV, skip commercials, and learn what you like. It never explained **why** it existed or what it stood for. Most people did not understand why they needed it, and sales stayed disappointing. Later, cable companies offered their own recorders, and TiVo's lead faded.

Sinek connects this to the **Law of Diffusion of Innovations**. A small group of **innovators and early adopters** (together about 15 percent of a market) buy new things because they share the belief behind them. The **early majority** only follows once enough early adopters have bought in. Sinek argues that mass-market success comes from reaching roughly **15 to 18 percent** of the market first, and you only get there by appealing to people who believe what you believe. TiVo's feature-based pitch never won that group over in a way that spread.

## The Lesson
**A better product is not enough.** To spread, you must first win the believers, and they respond to *why*, not to a feature list.

## When to Use This
- You have a great product but slow adoption
- You're targeting "the mass market" before winning early fans
- Your launch materials are mostly specs and features
- You're wondering why a weaker competitor is winning

## Try This
- Identify your early adopters: who would buy this because they share your belief, not because of a discount?
- Rewrite your product page so the first line explains why it exists.
