# The Pentagon's First Financial War Game

**One-line hook:** The U.S. military, used to planning for tanks and missiles, sat down to rehearse a war fought with currencies, stocks and bonds.

## The Story
Rickards opens the book with a war game he helped design and run in **2009** at a secure lab used by the Pentagon. Instead of soldiers and ships, the weapons were **financial**. Teams represented the United States, China, Russia and other players. Each could use tools like buying or dumping government bonds, manipulating currencies, cutting off access to markets or building gold reserves.

The exercise showed how **economic tools could be used as weapons** between rival nations. In Rickards' telling, some of the most worrying moves came from teams quietly building up gold and using financial dependence to pressure opponents. The national security experts in the room started to see that a country could be badly damaged without a shot being fired, simply through attacks on its **currency and financial system**.

Rickards uses the war game to set up his main argument: currencies and financial markets are now a **front line of national power**, and the world was entering a new period of "currency wars."

## The Lesson
Money is not just economics; it's **power**. Whoever controls key financial chokepoints can hurt rivals without military force.

## When to Use This
- You're trying to understand sanctions, trade fights or currency moves in the news
- Your business depends on a single currency, bank or payment system
- You're assessing risk in a global supply chain

## Try This
- List the financial chokepoints your business relies on (banks, currencies, payment networks) and imagine each one cut off.
- Ask how a rival or government could use your financial dependencies against you.
