# You Don't Need to Be a Genius. You Need to Be a Hunter.

**One-line hook:** The best ideas usually aren't invented from nothing; they're found by people who keep looking in more places than everyone else.

## The Story
*Note: this page summarizes the book's core idea rather than a specific anecdote. Deep should check it against the book and add a concrete example.*

The central argument of The Idea Hunter is that great ideas rarely come from a lone genius having a flash of inspiration. Instead, **ideas are out there in the world**, and the people who come up with the best ones are **hunters**: they actively and constantly search for ideas, and they don't care where those ideas come from.

The authors contrast this with the myth of the creative genius. If you believe good ideas come only from special people, you'll wait for inspiration or leave innovation to "the creative types." If you see yourself as a hunter, idea-finding becomes a **habit anyone can build**: stay curious, notice what others are doing, and **borrow and recombine** ideas from other fields, industries, and cultures. A hunter treats the whole world as a source, not just their own company or specialty.

This flips the usual question from "How do I become more creative?" to **"Where am I looking, and how often?"**

## The Lesson
Creativity is less about talent and more about **search**. The wider and more regularly you hunt, the more good ideas you'll find.

## When to Use This
- You're stuck and waiting for a "big idea" to arrive
- You think innovation is someone else's job
- Your team only looks at competitors in its own industry for ideas
- You feel less creative than your peers

## Try This
- Each week, study one idea from a field completely unrelated to yours and ask how it could apply to your work.
- Keep a running "idea file" of anything interesting you see, and review it monthly.
