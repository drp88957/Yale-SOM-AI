# Don't Ask a Brickmaker About Jewels

**One-line hook:** Arkad's first year of savings vanished because he trusted the wrong expert.

## The Story
After a year of saving one-tenth of his earnings, young **Arkad** wanted to invest. He gave his savings to **Azmur, a brickmaker**, who was traveling to buy rare jewels from the **Phoenicians**. The plan was to sell them in Babylon for a profit.

The Phoenicians cheated Azmur. The "jewels" turned out to be **worthless pieces of glass**, and Arkad lost everything. When he told Algamish, the moneylender laughed at him: why would you trust a brickmaker's knowledge about jewels? You would go to a **jeweler** for advice about jewels, just as you'd ask a brickmaker about bricks.

Arkad learned the lesson and started over. This mistake appears again in the book's **laws of gold**: gold slips away from those who invest in businesses they don't understand, or follow advice from people who are not skilled in that field.

## The Lesson
**Take advice only from people who know the field.** Confidence and good intentions are not expertise.

## When to Use This
- A friend or relative pitches an investment
- Getting advice outside someone's area of experience
- Hiring someone for a job they haven't done before
- Evaluating "hot tips" online or at work

## Try This
- Before acting on advice, ask: "Has this person actually done this successfully, many times?"
- Keep a rule: never invest in something you can't explain in two sentences.
