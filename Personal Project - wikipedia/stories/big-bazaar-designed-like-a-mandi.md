# Big Bazaar: A Supermarket That Felt Like a Bazaar

**One-line hook:** Biyani made his stores less neat on purpose, because that's how Indians like to shop.

## The Story
When modern retail came to India, the obvious model was the **Western supermarket**: clean aisles, neat shelves, quiet shopping. Kishore Biyani thought this felt foreign to most Indian shoppers. They were used to **crowded markets (mandis)**, noise, bargains, and touching and checking goods before buying.

So with **Big Bazaar**, he designed stores to feel familiar. They were busy and full, with piles of products and a sense of a market. In **Food Bazaar**, staples such as grains and pulses could be shown loose in open bins so customers could **see and feel** them, as they would at a local shop. The goal was a store where an ordinary Indian family felt at home, not out of place.

Biyani says much of his understanding came from **watching customers**, not from foreign consultants or textbooks. He wanted "Indian" retail, not a copy of the West.

## The Lesson
Don't copy a model that worked elsewhere. **Design for how your customers actually behave**, even if it looks "less professional" to outsiders.

## When to Use This
- Bringing a product or process from one country or culture to another
- Your polished design isn't connecting with real users
- Competing with a big, foreign or "best practice" player
- Choosing between what experts recommend and what users do

## Try This
- Watch real customers use your product or service for an hour. Note what they touch, avoid and ask about.
- Pick one "best practice" you follow and ask if it fits your users or just looks good.
