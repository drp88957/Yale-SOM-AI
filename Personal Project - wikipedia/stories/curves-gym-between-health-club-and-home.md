# Curves: The Gym With No Mirrors and No Men

**One-line hook:** Curves found its market by asking why women chose home workout videos over gyms, then combining the best of both.

## The Story
Health clubs competed on the same things: fancy equipment, classes, amenities, and attractive locations. But many women found these gyms **expensive, intimidating, and inconvenient**. Many chose the alternative instead: exercising at home with videos, which was cheap and private but lonely and hard to stick with.

**Curves**, a women-only fitness chain, looked **across these two alternatives** rather than within the gym industry. It kept what women valued from each and dropped the rest. From gyms, it kept a dedicated place to work out with **support from other women**. From home exercise, it kept **low cost, privacy, and convenience**.

It **eliminated** mirrors, men, complicated machines, and extras like pools and spas. It offered a simple **30-minute circuit workout** in small, neighborhood locations. Because the setup was so simple, it was cheap to run and spread quickly. The authors present it as an example of reconstructing market boundaries by **looking across alternative industries**.

## The Lesson
Your real competition is often not your rivals but the **alternatives** customers choose instead. Study why people pick those alternatives, and you may find a new market between them.

## When to Use This
- You're stuck comparing your product only to direct competitors
- Customers are choosing a "do it yourself" option instead of you
- Your offer feels intimidating to beginners
- You're designing a service and want to cut cost without cutting value

## Try This
- List what your customers do *instead* of using you. Write down why they choose it.
- Combine the top reason people pick you with the top reason they pick the alternative.
