# Believe: Why the SEALs Had to Fight Alongside Iraqi Soldiers

**One-line hook:** Leaders were ordered to take poorly trained Iraqi soldiers on combat missions, and nobody could lead it well until they understood why.

## The Story
In Ramadi, SEAL leaders were told they had to bring Iraqi Army soldiers on their operations. Many Iraqi troops were poorly trained and poorly equipped, and some might not be trustworthy. To the SEALs it seemed to make missions **more dangerous for no benefit**. Their instinct was to resist.

The book's point is that a leader cannot lead an order he doesn't believe in. So the leader **stepped back and asked "why?"** The bigger picture was strategic: the US would not stay forever. For Iraq to become stable, Iraqi forces had to learn to fight and secure their own country. Working alongside them was the way out. Once the leader understood that, he could explain it to his men honestly. The team then committed to the mission instead of just going through the motions.

## The Lesson
**If you don't understand or believe in a mission, ask up the chain until you do.** Then pass that "why" down. People follow conviction, not orders read aloud.

## When to Use This
- You're handed a company decision you privately disagree with
- Your team is complying with a change but not committing to it
- You need to explain a strategy shift to people who don't see the reason
- You're about to say "management wants this" to your team

## Try This
- Before passing a directive on, write one sentence: "We're doing this because..." If you can't, go ask your boss.
- Replace "they want us to" with "we're doing this so that..." in your next team update.
