# The Cosmetics Company: Too Much Stock and Still Running Out

**One-line hook:** A company had warehouses packed with product and still couldn't keep popular items on store shelves.

## The Story
One of Alex Rogo's companies makes cosmetics and ships them out to **regional warehouses**. Each warehouse holds a lot of stock, yet stores keep running out of the items people actually want, while unpopular items sit for months. The usual fix would be better forecasts or even more inventory.

Using Jonah's thinking processes, Alex and his team see the real problem: guessing demand for each region far in advance is almost impossible, and once product is pushed to the wrong region, it's stuck there. Their answer is to **keep most of the stock at a central warehouse** near the plant, where demand from all regions evens out. Regional warehouses hold much less and are **refilled based on what actually sold**, often and in small amounts.

The result is **less total inventory and fewer stockouts** at the same time, which most managers assumed was impossible. This also frees up cash and makes the company more valuable.

## The Lesson
**Push systems based on forecasts fail; pull systems based on real use work better.** Keep flexibility where uncertainty is lowest, and send things out only when they're needed.

## When to Use This
- You have too much of the wrong stuff and too little of the right stuff
- Forecasts keep missing and everyone blames the forecasters
- You're designing how resources get shared across teams or locations
- Budgets are handed out early and then get stuck in the wrong places

## Try This
- Hold back a central pool (stock, budget, or people) and give it out based on actual use, not upfront guesses.
- Shorten the refill cycle: smaller, more frequent replenishment beats big batches.
