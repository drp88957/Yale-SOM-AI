# The Matchsticks-and-Dice Game: Why "Average" Plans Fall Behind

**One-line hook:** A simple game with a die and some matchsticks shows why a plan that works "on average" still ends up late.

## The Story
During the scout hike, Alex Rogo sets up a game for the boys. They sit in a row with bowls between them. Each boy rolls a die and moves that many matchsticks from the bowl on his left to the bowl on his right, but only if the matchsticks are there. The die averages 3.5, so everyone expects the line to move about 3.5 matchsticks per turn.

It doesn't. Matchsticks **pile up** in some bowls, and the last boy falls further and further **behind the average**. The reason is the mix of two things: **dependent events** (each step relies on the step before it) and **statistical fluctuations** (the dice rolls vary). A high roll downstream can't use matchsticks that never arrived, so the slow rolls add up while the fast rolls are capped.

Alex realizes this is exactly what happens in his plant. Balancing every machine to the same "average" capacity does not create smooth flow. It creates growing inventory and late orders.

## The Lesson
**In a chain of steps, delays add up but gains don't.** Plans based on averages fail. You need buffers and spare capacity in the right places, especially in front of the constraint.

## When to Use This
- Your project plan assumes every task will take its "expected" time
- A multi-handoff process keeps slipping even though each step looks fine
- Someone wants to cut all slack to make every resource 100% busy
- You're estimating timelines for dependent work

## Try This
- Add a protective time buffer in front of the most critical step, not padding on every task.
- Track where work piles up across handoffs, not just how long each step takes.
