# Amazon's Fire Phone Flop That Led to the Echo

**One-line hook:** Amazon's expensive smartphone failure didn't end in blame; the lessons fed into one of its biggest hits.

## The Story
In 2014 Amazon launched the **Fire Phone**, its attempt to compete with the iPhone and Android. It flopped. Customers weren't interested, and Amazon had to write off a large amount of unsold inventory. By most standards it was an embarrassing, costly failure.

Jeff Bezos didn't treat it as a disaster to hide or punish. Amazon kept the team and the lessons. Experience from that effort fed into later products, including the **Echo** and its voice assistant **Alexa**, which became a major success. Bezos has long argued that if you're going to invent, you must be willing to fail, and that big wins pay for many failed experiments.

Varol uses Amazon as an example of **failing forward**. Organizations that punish failure teach people to only try safe ideas. Organizations that study failure and reuse what they learned can take the bigger swings that lead to breakthroughs. The key is that failure is **studied and used**, not celebrated for its own sake.

## The Lesson
A failed project can be the raw material for your next success, but only if you keep the people, study what went wrong, and don't punish the attempt.

## When to Use This
- A product launch or project just failed publicly
- Your team is afraid to propose bold ideas
- Deciding how to respond when someone's experiment didn't work
- Building a culture that encourages innovation

## Try This
- After a failure, hold a blameless review: what did we learn, and where else could it be used?
- Separate a bad outcome from a bad decision: ask whether the bet was reasonable given what you knew.
