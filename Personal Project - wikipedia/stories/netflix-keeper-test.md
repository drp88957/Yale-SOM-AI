# The Keeper Test: "Would You Fight to Keep Them?"

**One-line hook:** Netflix managers ask one blunt question about each person on their team, and it changes how they think about loyalty.

## The Story
Netflix says it is **a team, not a family**. A family keeps you no matter how you perform; a pro sports team wants the best player in every position. To keep talent density high, Netflix managers use the **Keeper Test**: *"If this person told me they were leaving for a similar job at another company, would I fight hard to change their mind?"* If the honest answer is no, the manager should give that person a **generous severance** now, so a stronger person can take the role.

Hastings admits this sounds harsh, and the book includes the discomfort and fear it created among employees. Netflix tried to soften it in a few ways: the severance is generous so no one is left stranded, and employees are encouraged to ask their own boss the Keeper Test about themselves ("How hard would you fight to keep me?"), so there are fewer surprises.

## The Lesson
**Being clear about performance is kinder than keeping someone in limbo.** The question also forces a manager to admit what they already know.

## When to Use This
- You keep "managing around" someone instead of addressing the issue
- You're reviewing your team before planning the next year
- You want to know where you yourself stand with your boss
- You're deciding whether a role is worth refilling as it is

## Try This
- Run the Keeper Test quietly on each person you manage, and note what you'd do for each "no."
- Ask your own manager: "If I said I was leaving, how hard would you work to keep me, and what would change your answer?"
