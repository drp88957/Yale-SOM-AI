# India One, India Two, India Three

**One-line hook:** Biyani got rich by building for the India most businesses ignored.

## The Story
Kishore Biyani split Indian consumers into rough groups. **India One** was the well-off, consuming class that most modern brands chased. **India Two** were the people who serve India One, such as drivers, maids, and small workers, with growing incomes and aspirations. **India Three** was everyone else, with the least spending power.

Most modern retailers aimed at India One. Biyani saw that **India Two** was huge, wanted to shop better, and was poorly served. Big Bazaar was built largely with this customer in mind: good value, familiar feel, and aspiration without intimidation. Serving India Two meant big volume at low prices, and a store where a domestic worker and her employer might both shop.

This framing came from Biyani's habit of observing people and his belief that India's consumers could not be understood through Western market categories.

## The Lesson
**Segment the market by how people really live**, not by standard labels. The largest opportunity is often the group everyone else overlooks.

## When to Use This
- Choosing a target customer for a new product
- Everyone in your industry chases the same premium buyer
- Making sense of market data that looks flat
- Pricing for a broad, aspiring middle market

## Try This
- Draw your own "India One / Two / Three" for your market. Who is underserved?
- Talk to five people from the group your competitors ignore, and ask what they'd want.
