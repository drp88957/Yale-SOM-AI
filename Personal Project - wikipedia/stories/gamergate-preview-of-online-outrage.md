# Gamergate: The Warning Sign Everyone Ignored

**One-line hook:** In 2014, a harassment campaign about video games turned out to be a preview of how online outrage would later shape politics.

## The Story
Fisher describes **Gamergate**, which began in 2014 when an ex-boyfriend of a game developer, **Zoe Quinn**, posted a long blog post about her. It spread across forums like 4chan and Reddit and grew into a huge, coordinated campaign of harassment against women in the gaming world, including threats and the publishing of personal information.

At the time, many people saw it as a strange fight inside a niche community. Fisher argues it was much more: a **preview** of how social platforms could bind angry, isolated people into a movement through shared grievance and a feeling of being under attack. The platforms' features (viral sharing, rewards for outrage, communities that reinforced each other) helped it spread. Fisher traces how tactics and people involved in Gamergate fed into the wider online culture wars and far-right movements that followed.

## The Lesson
Small, ugly patterns in a niche corner often **signal what's coming at scale**. Treat early warning signs seriously, especially when a system rewards anger.

## When to Use This
- A toxic pattern appears in a small part of your community or company
- You're tempted to dismiss an online backlash as "just a few trolls"
- You're moderating or designing an online space
- You want to understand how online movements form

## Try This
- Keep a "weak signals" log: odd behaviour in a corner of your community that could spread. Review it monthly.
- Set clear, enforced norms early; they are much harder to impose once a culture hardens.
