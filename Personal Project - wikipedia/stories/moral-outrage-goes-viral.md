# Why Moral Outrage Is the Fastest Thing Online

**One-line hook:** Researchers found that posts packed with moral and emotional words travel further, so the internet trains all of us to be angrier.

## The Story
Fisher discusses research by psychologists including **William Brady** and **Molly Crockett** on why some posts spread and others don't. Studying large numbers of social media posts on hot political topics, they found that messages using **moral-emotional language** (words that express outrage, disgust or blame) were shared significantly more than neutral ones.

Fisher connects this to how platforms work. When outrage gets more likes and shares, people learn, often without noticing, to post more outrage. Crockett's work suggests that online, expressing outrage is cheap and highly rewarded, while the real-world social costs that usually limit it are weaker. The result is a **feedback loop**: algorithms amplify outrage, users learn that outrage gets rewarded, and the whole conversation becomes angrier and more divided.

## The Lesson
**Outrage spreads faster than calm.** Every system that rewards attention will drift toward anger unless something pushes back.

## When to Use This
- You're writing a message and tempted to add anger to make it spread
- Your team chat or community is getting more heated over time
- You're judging how "everyone" feels from what's loudest online
- You're designing a feedback or review system

## Try This
- Before posting or sending something heated, wait an hour and reread it. Ask if it's meant to persuade or just to be shared.
- When reading online reactions, remember you're seeing the most outraged slice, not the average person.
