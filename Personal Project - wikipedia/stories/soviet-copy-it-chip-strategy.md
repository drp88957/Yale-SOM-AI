# "Copy It": Why the Soviet Union Could Never Catch Up in Chips

**One-line hook:** The Soviets stole American chips and copied them carefully, and that strategy guaranteed they would always be behind.

## The Story
During the Cold War, the Soviet Union wanted its own semiconductor industry. It built a science city near Moscow and used spies to obtain American chips and designs. Soviet leaders then told their engineers to **copy them**, rather than invent their own.

Miller argues this was a fatal mistake. Chips were improving at the pace of **Moore's Law**, getting dramatically better every couple of years. By the time Soviet engineers had studied and reproduced an American chip, Silicon Valley had moved on to a newer generation. Copying also meant the Soviets never built the deep know-how, supplier networks and culture of experimentation that drove progress in the US. Their industry stayed permanently **a few years behind**, which mattered greatly for computers and military systems.

## The Lesson
In a fast-moving field, **copying the leader locks you into second place**. To catch up, you have to build the capability that produces the next version, not just reproduce the current one.

## When to Use This
- Your strategy is "do what the market leader does, but cheaper"
- You're learning a skill by imitating experts without understanding why they do it
- Your team relies on reverse-engineering instead of developing its own expertise
- You're in a market where the technology changes every year or two

## Try This
- When you copy something, also ask "What process produced this?" and try to build that process.
- Spend some fixed share of effort on original experiments, even if most fail.
