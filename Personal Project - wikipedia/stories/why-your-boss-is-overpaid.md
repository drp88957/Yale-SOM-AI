# Why Your Boss Is "Overpaid" (On Purpose)

**One-line hook:** The CEO's giant salary may not be a reward for the CEO at all. It's bait for everyone below.

## The Story
Why does a chief executive earn so much more than the vice presidents just below, even when the work doesn't seem that different? Harford explains **tournament theory**.

In many organizations, it's hard to measure exactly how well each person is doing. But it's easy to tell **who is doing better than whom**. So companies run a contest. The top job comes with a huge prize, and that prize is meant to motivate the **many people competing for it**. The CEO's pay is like the prize money in a golf tournament. It's not about how much harder the winner played on the final hole. It's about making every player try hard all season.

Harford also shows the **downside**. Tournaments encourage people to take big gambles (because coming second pays little), and they can reward **sabotaging rivals** instead of helping colleagues. Where teamwork matters most, a big, all-or-nothing prize can do real damage.

## The Lesson
Big gaps in pay or status often work as **prizes to drive competition** below. That can spur effort, but it can also breed risk-taking and backstabbing.

## When to Use This
- You're designing a promotion or bonus system
- Your team competes when you need it to cooperate
- You're deciding whether to chase a "winner-take-all" promotion

## Try This
- If teamwork matters, reward team results and narrow the gap between first and second place.
- Before entering a tournament, ask what the "losers" get and whether you're okay with that.
