# Growing Broke: How a Japanese Trading Company Saved Nike

**One-line hook:** Nike's sales kept doubling, and that's exactly why it kept running out of money.

## The Story
In its early years, Phil Knight's company grew fast, but **every dollar went back into more inventory**. Knight believed in "grow or die," so he kept ordering more shoes than the business could easily afford. His banks grew nervous about a company with **almost no cash cushion**. Knight describes being lectured by bankers to slow down, and the constant fear of not meeting the next payment.

The crisis came when his bank **cut him off** and even treated his cash juggling as possible fraud. Knight thought it might be the end. What saved him was a relationship he had built with the Japanese trading company **Nissho Iwai**, which had been financing his shoe orders. Nissho stepped in and **paid off the bank**, standing behind Knight when American lenders wouldn't.

Knight credits people at Nissho who trusted him and his team. That trust came from years of honest dealing and personal relationships, not from the balance sheet.

## The Lesson
**Fast growth eats cash.** Profitable on paper isn't the same as having money in the bank, and strong relationships can carry you when the numbers can't.

## When to Use This
- Your project or business is growing faster than its funding
- You're deciding how aggressively to expand
- You need allies before a crisis, not during one
- You're managing personal money tied up in illiquid things

## Try This
- Build a simple cash forecast for the next few months and mark the tightest week.
- Invest in one key relationship (a lender, partner, or mentor) now, before you need help.
