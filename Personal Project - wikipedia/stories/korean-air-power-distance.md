# Korean Air & the Plane Crashes Caused by Politeness

**One-line hook:** Planes crashed because junior pilots were too polite to tell the captain he was wrong.

## The Story
In the 1990s Korean Air had one of the worst crash rates in the industry. Gladwell argues that the cause was usually not bad flying. It was **communication shaped by culture**. In cockpit recordings, first officers who saw danger coming hinted at it ("mitigated speech") rather than saying it outright. In a high **power-distance** culture, telling the captain plainly that he was making a mistake felt unthinkable.

The turnaround came when the airline brought in outside help from Delta and made **English the cockpit language**. Speaking English gave crews a way to step out of the deference built into Korean. Korean Air later became a safe airline.

## The Lesson
Failures are often **communication failures that look like skill failures**. The hierarchy in a room can quietly overrule the facts in that room.

## When to Use This
- A team keeps missing problems that someone "must have seen"
- You're the senior person and nobody pushes back on you
- You're working across cultures or with very hierarchical organizations
- You're designing a process for escalation or safety

## Try This
- Make pushback easy: "Tell me the one thing I'm getting wrong."
- Set a shared, neutral protocol (like checklists or a "stop the line" rule) so speaking up doesn't depend on courage.
