# A Few Close Relationships Give You Most of the Value

**One-line hook:** Koch suggests that, like profits, the value in our relationships comes mostly from a small number of people.

## The Story
Koch extends the 80/20 Principle to **relationships**. He argues that most of the value we get from our friendships, family ties, and professional connections comes from **a small number of close relationships**. Yet many people spread their time thinly across a large circle of acquaintances, giving each a little attention and none enough.

He encourages readers to **list their most important relationships** and honestly assess which ones matter most, both personally and at work. Then put **more time and care into those few**, rather than trying to keep up with everyone equally. He applies the same thinking to professional allies: a handful of strong working relationships, such as key mentors, colleagues, and partners, can matter more to a career than a big network of weak contacts.

The point is not to drop people coldly, but to be **deliberate**: recognize that deep relationships take time, and time is limited, so give it where it creates the most value for both sides.

## The Lesson
**Depth beats breadth in relationships.** Invest generously in the few people who matter most, because that's where most of the value, and happiness, comes from.

## When to Use This
- You feel stretched by social and networking obligations
- You're thinking about who to ask for career advice or support
- An important relationship has been neglected because you're busy
- You're planning how to spend scarce free time

## Try This
- Write down the people who matter most to your life and career. Check when you last gave each of them real time.
- Schedule one meaningful conversation this week with someone on that list.
