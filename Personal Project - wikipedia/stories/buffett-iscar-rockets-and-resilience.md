# Buffett's First Foreign Buy: The Israeli Factory That Kept Working Under Rocket Fire

**One-line hook:** Warren Buffett, famous for never investing abroad, bought an Israeli company, and a war soon tested his bet.

## The Story
Start-up Nation opens with **Warren Buffett**, who for decades had avoided buying whole companies outside the United States. In 2006, Berkshire Hathaway agreed to buy most of **Iscar**, an Israeli maker of precision cutting tools, run by the Wertheimer family. It was Buffett's **first major acquisition outside the U.S.**

Soon after, the **2006 war with Hezbollah** broke out, and rockets fell across northern Israel, including near Iscar's main plant in the Galilee. Many employees had to shelter or were called up for military service. Yet the company **kept production going**, and the workers who remained kept the plant running. Customers kept receiving orders.

Buffett visited Israel after the war and stood by his investment. For the authors, the story captures the puzzle of the book: how does a small country under constant threat produce companies that global investors trust with their money?

## The Lesson
**Resilience under pressure is one of the strongest signals of trust you can send.** What a team does in a crisis tells partners more than any pitch.

## When to Use This
- You're judging a partner, supplier, or team's reliability
- Your team is working through a crisis and wondering if it's worth the effort
- You're assessing risk in an unfamiliar country or market
- You want to understand why investors back certain places despite danger

## Try This
- Ask a potential partner: "Tell me about the worst disruption you've faced and what you did."
- After a crisis, tell your key customers exactly how you kept your promises. It builds lasting trust.
