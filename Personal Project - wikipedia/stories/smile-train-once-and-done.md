# Smile Train's "Give Once and We'll Never Ask Again"

**One-line hook:** A charity raised more money by promising donors it would stop asking them for money.

## The Story
**Smile Train** is a charity that pays for surgery to repair cleft lips and palates in poor countries. Like most charities, it sent repeated fundraising letters, and like most donors, people got tired of being asked over and over.

Working with Levitt and Dubner, Smile Train tested an unusual offer: **give a donation now, and we'll never ask you again**. It sounded like a way to lose future income. Instead, **more people gave**. And surprisingly few donors actually used the option to be left alone, so Smile Train kept most of its future giving too.

The authors explain why this worked. Donors felt the charity **respected** them. Unlike every other mailing, this one offered them something they wanted: an end to the nagging. A surprising offer also got their attention and made the request stand out.

## The Lesson
Look at the situation through the other person's eyes. Sometimes the best way to get what you want is to **offer people what they really want**, even if it seems to cut against your own interests.

## When to Use This
- You keep "following up" and people stop responding
- You're writing a fundraising, sales or recruiting pitch
- Customers are tired of your emails or calls

## Try This
- Ask, "What does the other person dislike about how we approach them?" and remove it.
- Test a surprising offer (fewer contacts, an easy opt-out) on a small group and measure the result.
