# The Founders Who Won by Thinking Like Scientists

**One-line hook:** Startup founders taught to treat their strategy as a hypothesis made more money than those who just trusted their vision.

## The Story
Grant describes a study led by **Arnaldo Camuffo** and colleagues with early-stage entrepreneurs in Italy. All the founders got training on how to build a business. Half were also taught to **think like scientists**: treat their strategy as a **theory**, turn customer interviews into ways to **test hypotheses**, and use experiments to decide what to do.

Over the following year, the founders trained in scientific thinking brought in **much more revenue** on average than the others. They were also **more likely to pivot** when the evidence told them their idea wasn't working, rather than stubbornly sticking with their original plan.

Grant uses this to introduce his main framework. When we hold opinions, we often slip into one of three modes: **preacher** (defending our sacred beliefs), **prosecutor** (attacking others' ideas), or **politician** (seeking approval). The better mode is the **scientist**: hold beliefs as hypotheses and look for evidence that might prove you wrong.

## The Lesson
Treat your plan as a hypothesis to test, not a vision to defend. The willingness to pivot based on evidence is a competitive advantage.

## When to Use This
- Starting a new product, venture, or side project
- Your team is debating strategy based on opinions
- You notice yourself defending an idea instead of testing it
- Deciding whether to pivot or persevere

## Try This
- Write your key assumption as a testable hypothesis and run the cheapest possible experiment on it this week.
- In meetings, ask "What evidence would change our minds?" before debating.
