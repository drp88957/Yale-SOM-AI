# Why Drive-Up ATMs Have Braille

**One-line hook:** Blind people don't drive, so why do drive-through cash machines have Braille on the keypad?

## The Story
Frank's book is built on questions his students asked about everyday puzzles, and this is one of the most famous. Drive-up ATMs are used by people sitting in cars. Blind people can't drive. So why go to the trouble of putting **Braille** on the keys?

The answer is simple **cost-benefit** thinking. ATM makers need Braille keypads for walk-up machines anyway, which blind people do use. Making **one standard keypad** for all machines is cheaper than making and stocking two different versions. Adding Braille to a keypad costs almost nothing once you're already producing it. Using the same part everywhere also avoids mix-ups during installation.

What looked like a silly waste is actually the **cheaper option**.

## The Lesson
When something seems pointless, look at the **costs and benefits from the producer's side**. Standardizing is often cheaper than customizing, even if some features go unused.

## When to Use This
- A process or product has a feature that seems pointless
- You're deciding between one standard design and several custom ones
- You're tempted to remove something "nobody uses"

## Try This
- Before cutting a "useless" feature, ask what it costs to keep and what it would cost to make a version without it.
- Look for places where one standard version could replace several customized ones.
