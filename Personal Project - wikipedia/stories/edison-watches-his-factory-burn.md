# Edison Watches His Factory Burn and Calls His Family

**One-line hook:** When fire destroyed much of his life's work, Edison told his son to bring his mother to watch.

## The Story
In **1914**, a fire broke out at **Thomas Edison's** research and manufacturing complex in West Orange, New Jersey. Buildings, records and prototypes went up in flames. Edison was in his late sixties. Much of the damage was not covered by insurance.

Holiday tells how Edison, instead of breaking down, calmly found his son and told him to go get his mother and her friends, because **they would never see a fire like this again**. When his son was worried, Edison's attitude was that the fire had wiped out a lot of rubbish and they could start fresh.

The next day he got to work rebuilding. Holiday uses the story to show **acceptance**: what has happened cannot be undone, so you might as well accept it and **turn your energy to what's next**. Edison's team got back to production remarkably quickly.

## The Lesson
You can't control the disaster, but you **can control your response**. Accepting what happened quickly frees your energy to rebuild.

## When to Use This
- A project you worked on for months fails or gets cancelled
- You lose data, a client or a job
- Your team is shaken by a big setback
- You catch yourself replaying "if only" scenarios

## Try This
- Say out loud: "This happened. What's the first thing I can do tomorrow?"
- After a setback, list what the loss made room for.
