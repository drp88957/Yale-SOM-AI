# The NCX-10: An Hour Lost at the Bottleneck Is an Hour Lost for Everyone

**One-line hook:** The plant's most important machine was sitting idle at lunch, and nobody thought that mattered.

## The Story
Once Alex Rogo's team finds their bottlenecks, an expensive **NCX-10** machine and the **heat-treat** department, Jonah visits and walks the floor with them. They discover the NCX-10 is **idle during lunch breaks and shift changes**. Some parts reach it that will later be **scrapped for defects**, so bottleneck time is spent on bad parts. Heat-treat is processing parts that aren't needed for any current order.

Jonah's rule: **an hour lost at a bottleneck is an hour lost for the whole system**. An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage. The team makes changes: they keep the bottleneck running through breaks, move **quality checks in front of** the bottleneck so it only works on good parts, mark bottleneck parts so they get **priority**, and bring back an older machine (the Zmegma) to take some load off the NCX-10. Throughput goes up without major new spending.

These steps become Goldratt's way of improving: **identify** the constraint, **exploit** it, **subordinate** everything else to it, **elevate** it, and then **repeat** when the constraint moves.

## The Lesson
**Protect and fully use your constraint before spending money on more capacity.** Most bottlenecks waste a lot of their time on things that don't matter.

## When to Use This
- Your scarcest expert spends time on admin or low-value work
- You're about to buy or hire capacity to fix a slow process
- Rework is landing on your busiest person or step
- The bottleneck sits idle while everyone else is busy

## Try This
- Watch your bottleneck for a day and log every minute it's idle or doing work that will be thrown away.
- Put a quality check right before the bottleneck so it only handles good inputs.
