# The NCX-10: An Hour Lost at the Bottleneck Is an Hour Lost for Everyone

**One-line hook:** The plant's most important machine was sitting idle at lunch, and nobody thought that mattered.

## The Story
After the scout hike, Alex Rogo comes back to the plant with a new question for his team. Somewhere in Bearington there must be **Herbies**: resources that can't keep up with the demand placed on them. Jonah later gives these a name. A **bottleneck** is any resource whose capacity is equal to or less than the demand placed on it. A **non-bottleneck** is any resource whose capacity is greater than the demand on it. The team's first job is to find the bottlenecks.

They start with the plant's data, and it lets them down. The records on capacity and demand are messy and unreliable, and they don't clearly point to anything. So the team does something simpler. They **walk the floor** and look for the place where work piles up. On the trail, the gap opened up in front of Herbie. In a plant, the clue should be the same: huge stacks of parts waiting in front of a slow step.

Two places stand out. The first is the **NCX-10**, the expensive, automated machine that can do work which used to take several machines and several operators. The second is **heat treat**, the department with the furnaces where parts are baked to harden them. Both have mountains of parts waiting in front of them. Both handle many of the parts that go into the plant's late orders. These are Bearington's Herbies.

The team's first instinct is to add capacity. But there is no money to buy another NCX-10, and new furnaces would take too long. So Alex calls Jonah, and this time Jonah comes to the plant.

Jonah walks the floor with the team. When they reach the NCX-10, he finds it **standing idle**. Its crew is on a break. Like every other machine in the plant, it stops for lunch, for breaks and between shifts. Nobody sees a problem. Everyone takes breaks.

Jonah asks a pointed question. How much does an hour of the bottleneck cost? Lou, the controller, gives the answer from the cost accounting system. It's a modest figure: the cost of running that machine for an hour, including its share of overhead. Jonah tells him that's the wrong way to see it. The bottleneck sets the pace for the whole plant. Whatever the NCX-10 doesn't produce, the plant can't ship. So an hour of the NCX-10 isn't worth the cost of one machine for an hour. It's worth the output of the **entire plant for an hour**. When the team works it out that way, the cost of a lost hour is in the thousands of dollars.

This is where Jonah gives the book's most quoted rule: **an hour lost at a bottleneck is an hour lost for the total system. An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage.** An hour lost at a bottleneck can never be made up, because the bottleneck has no spare time to recover it. An hour saved somewhere else changes nothing, because those machines already have more capacity than they need.

The walk keeps turning up waste. Some parts that pass through the bottlenecks are later **scrapped**. Some were already defective before they reached the bottleneck. Others are damaged by careless handling afterward. Either way, precious bottleneck time has been thrown away on parts that will never be sold.

At heat treat, Jonah sees more of the same. The furnaces sometimes run with half-empty loads. At other times parts sit waiting because the workers who load and unload the furnace are busy somewhere else. A furnace can finish its cycle and then simply sit there until someone comes back.

Then Jonah finds the worst problem. The bottlenecks are working on parts for orders that aren't due for **months**, while orders that are already late wait in the pile. The plant releases work to keep machines busy and efficiency numbers high, not to meet what customers need now. The bottlenecks, the most precious time in the plant, are being spent on the wrong jobs.

Jonah doesn't hand out orders. As always, he asks questions and leaves the team to work out the answers. And they do. They arrange for the NCX-10 to **keep running through lunches and breaks**, so it no longer stops just because people are eating. They move **quality checks in front of the bottlenecks**, so only good parts use bottleneck time, and they take more care with parts after they come out. They make sure the bottlenecks work first on what is **actually due**, starting with the late orders. They take some load off the NCX-10 by bringing back older, slower machines, including one called the **Zmegma**, that can do some of the same work. For heat treat, they look at sending some work outside and at loading the furnaces more sensibly.

None of this costs much money. But throughput starts to rise, and late orders begin to ship.

## Details Worth Remembering
- A bottleneck is a resource whose capacity is equal to or less than the demand placed on it.
- The plant's data is unreliable, so the team finds the bottlenecks by seeing where work piles up.
- The two bottlenecks are the NCX-10 machine and heat treat.
- Jonah finds the NCX-10 idle during a break.
- Standard costing says an hour of the bottleneck is cheap. Jonah says it's worth an hour of the whole plant's output.
- "An hour lost at a bottleneck is an hour lost for the total system. An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage."
- Some parts that use bottleneck time are later scrapped.
- The bottlenecks are making parts for orders due months later while late orders wait.
- Fixes: run through breaks, quality checks before the bottleneck, work on what's due, and use the old Zmegma to share the load.

## The Lesson
**Protect and fully use your constraint before spending money on more capacity.** Most bottlenecks waste a lot of their time on breaks, bad inputs and work that isn't needed yet. Value bottleneck time at what it costs the whole system, not what it costs on its own.

## When to Use This
- Your scarcest expert spends time on admin or low-value work
- You're about to buy or hire capacity to fix a slow process
- Rework is landing on your busiest person or step
- The bottleneck sits idle while everyone else is busy
- Urgent work waits while the key resource handles things due much later

## Try This
- Watch your bottleneck for a day and log every minute it's idle or doing work that will be thrown away.
- Put a quality check right before the bottleneck so it only handles good inputs.
- Work out what one hour of your bottleneck is worth to the whole system. Use that figure when deciding whether to protect it.
