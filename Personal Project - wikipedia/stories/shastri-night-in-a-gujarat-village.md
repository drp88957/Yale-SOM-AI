# The Prime Minister Who Spent a Night in a Village

**One-line hook:** A Prime Minister skipped the official tour, talked to farmers for himself, and decided to copy Anand across India.

## The Story
In the mid-1960s, Prime Minister **Lal Bahadur Shastri** visited the Anand area. He wanted to understand why the Amul cooperative worked so well. Kurien describes how Shastri chose to **stay overnight in a village** and talk directly with farmers, without the usual official show.

What he saw convinced him. Farmers **owned** the cooperative, sold their milk to it, and got paid fairly and regularly. Professionals ran the operations, but the farmers were in charge. Shastri asked Kurien to help **replicate the "Anand pattern"** across the country. This led to the creation of the **National Dairy Development Board (NDDB)**, which Kurien led.

Kurien insisted the board be based in Anand, close to farmers, rather than in Delhi. He believed being near the people you serve keeps an institution honest and practical.

## The Lesson
The best way to understand something is to **see it at ground level, without the filter**. And when you scale a model, keep its center close to the people it serves.

## When to Use This
- You're making a decision based on reports and presentations only
- Deciding whether to copy a successful local model elsewhere
- Choosing where a team or headquarters should sit
- Persuading a senior leader: let them see the real thing

## Try This
- Spend time with the users or frontline team directly, without a guided tour.
- Before scaling, write down the two or three things that make the model work, and protect them.
