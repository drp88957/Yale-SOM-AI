# Martin Luther King Jr. Gave the "I Have a Dream" Speech, Not the "I Have a Plan" Speech

**One-line hook:** Around 250,000 people showed up in Washington without invitations or a website, because of what they believed.

## The Story
On **August 28, 1963**, about **250,000 people** gathered at the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C. to hear Martin Luther King Jr. Sinek points out that there were no invitations sent and no website to check the date. People came anyway.

Sinek's point is that they didn't come for King. **They came for themselves**, because they shared his belief about how America should be. King was not the only person who knew what needed to change, and he wasn't the only great speaker. But he was able to put into words a **belief** that millions already felt, and he talked about it in a way that let others take it on as their own.

That's why the speech is remembered as **"I have a dream,"** not "I have a plan." Plans tell people what to do; a dream tells them why it matters. King gave people something to believe in, and the people who believed told others.

## The Lesson
**Leaders inspire by sharing a belief others can adopt, not by issuing a plan.** When people make your why their own, they show up on their own.

## When to Use This
- You're trying to rally people who don't report to you
- Your team follows instructions but shows no energy
- You're giving a big speech or kickoff
- A movement or initiative depends on volunteers

## Try This
- Before your next presentation, write one sentence that starts with "I believe..." and open with it.
- Replace one step-by-step instruction in a team update with a short explanation of why the work matters.
