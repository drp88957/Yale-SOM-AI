# Why Milk Comes in Boxes and Soda in Cans

**One-line hook:** The shape of a container tells you a surprising amount about costs and trade-offs.

## The Story
Frank asks why **soft drinks** come in round cans and bottles while **milk** usually comes in rectangular cartons. Rectangles use shelf space more efficiently; round containers waste the gaps between them. So why aren't soda cans square too?

The answer is about **where the costs fall**. Milk must be kept in **refrigerated cases**, and refrigerated space is expensive to run. Packing milk tightly in rectangles saves a lot of costly cooled space. Soft drinks are mostly stored on ordinary, unrefrigerated shelves, where space is cheaper. So for soda, other benefits of a round can matter more: it's **comfortable to hold and drink from**, and cylinders handle the pressure of carbonation well. People also often drink soda straight from the can, while milk is usually poured into a glass.

The difference isn't random. Each shape is the best answer to a **different set of costs and benefits**.

## The Lesson
Design choices that look arbitrary often reflect **hidden trade-offs**. Find the most expensive constraint, and the design usually makes sense.

## When to Use This
- You're copying a design or process from another context
- You're deciding how to package, store or ship something
- You see a competitor do something "strange" and want to understand why

## Try This
- List the costliest constraint in your situation (space, time, people) and design around it first.
- Before copying a "best practice," check whether your costs and benefits are the same as theirs.
