# Marc Rich: The Trader Who Sold Iran's Oil to Israel

**One-line hook:** After the 1979 Iranian Revolution, one trader kept oil flowing between two sworn enemies, and became a fugitive in the process.

## The Story
**Marc Rich** was one of the founders of modern commodity trading. Blas and Farchy describe how he thrived where others feared to go. After the **Iranian Revolution** in 1979, Iran was cut off from many of its old buyers. Rich kept doing business with the new regime. Among the most striking deals, the authors describe how Iranian oil reached **Israel**, Iran's enemy, through Rich's network, including use of a secretive pipeline across Israel.

Rich made a fortune by acting as the **middleman in politically toxic places**. He traded with regimes that Western governments shunned and moved goods through sanctions and wars. The cost came later. In 1983 the US charged him with tax evasion and trading with Iran during the hostage crisis. Rich fled to **Switzerland** and spent years on the run, until President Bill Clinton controversially **pardoned** him on his last day in office in 2001. The trading houses that came out of his firm, including Glencore, still shape global markets.

## The Lesson
The biggest profits often sit **where others refuse to go**, but so do the biggest risks. Every edge built on going where others won't comes with a reputational and legal bill.

## When to Use This
- You're tempted by an opportunity others are avoiding for ethical or political reasons
- You're assessing a partner who seems to be able to "get anything done"
- You need to understand how sanctions are actually evaded
- You're weighing short-term gains against long-term reputation

## Try This
- For any "nobody else will do this" opportunity, write down why others are avoiding it, and decide if you accept that risk in daylight.
- Run the "front-page test": would you be comfortable seeing this deal described in a newspaper?
