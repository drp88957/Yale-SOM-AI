# PayPal vs. X.com: The Rivalry That Almost Sank Both

**One-line hook:** Two startups fought so hard over the same market that their best move was to stop fighting and merge.

## The Story
In late 1999, PayPal was in a fierce race with **X.com**, Elon Musk's online payments startup. The two companies were offering very similar products to the same customers, and both were spending heavily to win. Thiel describes an intense, almost obsessive rivalry, where the teams were focused on beating each other rather than on the bigger picture.

As the dot-com bubble began to look shaky, both sides realized they were in a battle that could leave them both broke. In early 2000 they **merged**. Together they were in a much better position to survive the crash and focus on building the business instead of fighting each other.

Thiel uses this story to show the **ideology of competition**. We are taught that competition is healthy and noble, but when rivals fight over the same thing, they **copy each other, lose sight of what matters, and destroy profits**. He compares it to Shakespeare's Montagues and Capulets: two houses "both alike" that end up fighting mainly because they are fighting.

## The Lesson
**Competition is often a trap, not a virtue.** When you are locked in a head-to-head war, step back and ask whether the fight is worth winning, or whether you should merge, partner, or move somewhere else.

## When to Use This
- You're obsessed with beating one rival and copying their every move
- Prices or margins in your market keep shrinking because of a feature war
- A rivalry at work is costing more than it is worth
- You're deciding whether to partner with a competitor

## Try This
- List your team's last five big decisions. How many were reactions to a competitor rather than to customers?
- Ask: "If we partnered with or ignored our rival, what would we do differently?"
