# Idea Hunting Is a Daily Habit, Not a Brainstorming Session

**One-line hook:** The best idea hunters don't wait for an offsite workshop; they gather ideas every day through curiosity, wide reading, and their network.

## The Story
*Note: this page summarizes the book's core ideas rather than a specific anecdote. Deep should check it against the book and add a concrete example.*

Boynton and Fischer describe idea hunting as an **ongoing way of working**, not an occasional event. Rather than relying on a once-a-year brainstorm, idea hunters build habits that keep a steady stream of ideas coming in. In broad terms, the book stresses being **curious** about many things, staying **well informed** about what's happening beyond your own area, keeping a **flexible, playful** attitude that lets you try ideas without getting stuck, and drawing on a **wide network of people** who expose you to new thinking.

The authors also emphasize that an idea is only worth something when it is **put to work**. Hunting is half the job; the other half is shaping the idea, testing it, and getting it used. Many ideas fail not because they are bad, but because no one does the work of turning them into something real.

The image is of a hunter who is always alert and always gathering, so that when a problem comes up, they already have a stock of ideas to draw from.

## The Lesson
**Build a daily system for collecting ideas, and a habit for putting the best ones into action.** Ideas compound when you hunt for them regularly.

## When to Use This
- Your team only comes up with new ideas in planned sessions
- You consume lots of information but rarely act on it
- Your professional network is narrow or all from one field
- You have good ideas that never get past the talking stage

## Try This
- Schedule one coffee a month with someone outside your industry, and ask what problem they're excited about.
- For each idea you save, write one small next step to test it within a week.
