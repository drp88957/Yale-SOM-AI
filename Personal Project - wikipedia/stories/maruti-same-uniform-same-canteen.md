# Same Uniform, Same Canteen: Maruti's Culture Shock

**One-line hook:** A government company in India got its bosses to wear the same uniform and eat in the same canteen as the workers.

## The Story
Indian public sector companies in that era were known for **hierarchy**: separate dining halls for officers, private cabins, and a large gap between managers and the shop floor. Maruti, working with Suzuki, chose to borrow **Japanese work practices** instead.

Bhargava describes how managers and workers wore the **same uniform**, ate in a **common canteen**, and sat in **open offices** instead of closed cabins. Discipline and punctuality were taken seriously at every level. Many Indian employees were also sent to Japan to train and see Suzuki's plants.

This was not just for show. Removing status symbols made it easier for workers and managers to talk about problems on the line. It also made quality and productivity everyone's job. Bhargava presents this culture as one of the main reasons Maruti could build cars at Japanese standards inside a government-owned company, which many people thought was impossible.

## The Lesson
Culture is shaped by **small visible symbols**. If you want people to feel like one team, remove the signs that tell them they are not.

## When to Use This
- Building or fixing culture in a hierarchical organization
- Leaders seem far away from frontline problems
- Bringing practices from one culture into another
- Starting a new team and choosing its norms

## Try This
- List the status symbols in your team (seating, titles, meetings only some people attend). Pick one to remove.
- Spend time where the work happens. Eat, sit or work next to the people doing it.
