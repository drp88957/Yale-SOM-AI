# Move 37: The Day a Machine Played Like No Human Ever Had

**One-line hook:** In 2016, an AI made a move in Go that experts called a mistake, and it turned out to be genius.

## The Story
In 2016, DeepMind's program **AlphaGo** played the world-class Go champion **Lee Sedol**. Go is an ancient game that humans have studied for many centuries. In the second game, AlphaGo played **Move 37**, a move so unusual that commentators thought it was an error. No strong human player would have chosen it. Yet it helped AlphaGo win, and the AI won the match.

Harari uses Move 37 to make a big point: AI is not just a faster tool, it is an **"alien" kind of intelligence**. It can find ideas that thousands of years of human tradition missed, and it can make decisions in ways we don't fully understand. That's exciting (new discoveries) and worrying (we may not be able to explain or check its choices). For Harari, AI is the first technology that can make decisions and create ideas **by itself**, which changes the nature of information networks.

## The Lesson
Expert consensus can hide huge blind spots. And when a system is smarter than you in some area, **understanding why it decides matters as much as whether it wins**.

## When to Use This
- You're adopting AI tools and deciding how much to trust their outputs
- Your field has "best practices" nobody has questioned in decades
- You're planning a career in a field AI might reshape
- An unconventional idea looks wrong to experts but has evidence behind it

## Try This
- When an AI (or a junior colleague) suggests something "wrong," test it before dismissing it.
- For any AI decision that affects people, require an explanation you could defend to someone else.
