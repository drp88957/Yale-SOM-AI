# Bowerman's Waffle Iron: A Breakthrough Made in the Kitchen

**One-line hook:** One of Nike's most famous soles was invented by pouring rubber into a family waffle iron.

## The Story
Phil Knight's partner, **Bill Bowerman**, was a legendary track coach at the University of Oregon, and he never stopped tinkering with shoes. He took apart running shoes, rebuilt them, and tested them on his own athletes, always trying to make them **lighter** and give **better grip**. His runners were his test lab.

One morning at breakfast, Bowerman looked at the pattern on his **waffle iron** and wondered if a sole with that grid of raised nubs would grip better while staying light. He poured urethane into his wife's waffle iron and **ruined it**, since the material stuck. But he kept experimenting until he had a working mold. The resulting **"waffle" sole** became the basis for a hit Nike shoe and a big part of the brand's early identity.

Knight presents Bowerman as obsessed with the athlete's performance, not with fashion or cost. That obsession, and a willingness to make a mess, gave Nike products competitors didn't have.

## The Lesson
**Breakthroughs often come from people who keep experimenting close to the real user.** Ideas can come from anywhere, even the breakfast table, if you're always looking.

## When to Use This
- Your product feels like everyone else's
- You're stuck and need a fresh angle
- You want to build a habit of small, quick experiments
- You have direct access to users but aren't learning from them

## Try This
- Run one cheap, messy prototype this week instead of planning a perfect one.
- Look at an everyday object and ask how its design could solve your problem.
