# Why Elite Hockey Players Are Born in January

**One-line hook:** Look at the roster of an elite Canadian hockey team and you'll see that most players were born in the first few months of the year.

## The Story
Gladwell points out that in elite Canadian hockey, a strikingly high share of players are born in **January, February and March**. Very few are born late in the year.

The reason is the **January 1 age cutoff**. A boy born in January can be almost a full year older than a boy in the same age group who was born in December. At age 9 or 10, that year means more size and coordination. The older kids look "more talented," get picked for all-star teams, and then get better coaching, more games and more practice. A small head start grows into a real gap in skill.

Gladwell calls this the **Matthew Effect**: "unto every one that hath shall be given."

## The Lesson
What looks like talent is often **an early advantage that got rewarded and then grew**. Selecting people too early tends to lock in luck rather than ability.

## When to Use This
- You're judging people on early performance (interns, juniors, first-year results)
- You're designing a selection, streaming or promotion process
- Someone "isn't a natural" and you're about to give up on them

## Try This
- Delay or repeat selection points, and give late bloomers a second look.
- Ask: "Is this person better, or just further along?"
