# The Magic of FREE: Truffles vs. Kisses

**One-line hook:** Cutting the price of two chocolates by the same one cent completely flipped which one people chose.

## The Story
Ariely and colleagues set up a table offering two chocolates: a fancy **Lindt truffle** for 15 cents, and a plain **Hershey's Kiss** for 1 cent. The truffle was the better deal relative to its usual price, and most people chose it.

Then they **lowered both prices by one cent**: the truffle cost 14 cents, and the Kiss was **free**. The difference between the two stayed exactly the same. Rationally, choices shouldn't change. But they did, dramatically. Now **most people chose the free Kiss**.

Ariely calls this the **power of FREE**. Zero isn't just another price. When something is free, we forget the downside and feel we can't lose. That emotional pull leads us to choose things we don't really want, like signing up for free offers, taking free samples, or choosing an option with free shipping even when it costs more overall.

## The Lesson
"Free" is not just a low price. It's an **emotional trigger** that can override careful thinking, for buyers and sellers alike.

## When to Use This
- You're designing a launch offer or trial
- You're deciding whether to charge a small fee or nothing
- You notice yourself grabbing something just because it's free

## Try This
- For a launch or trial, test a free option against a tiny price and compare uptake.
- Before accepting a "free" deal, ask what it really costs in time, attention or later spending.
