# No PowerPoint: Amazon's Six-Page Memos and Two-Pizza Teams

**One-line hook:** Bezos banned slide decks and made executives write full memos, then read them in silence at the start of meetings.

## The Story
Brad Stone describes how Jeff Bezos came to distrust **PowerPoint**. He felt bullet points let presenters hide fuzzy thinking and skip over the hard connections between ideas. So Amazon's senior meetings moved to **written narratives**, typically up to **six pages**, in full sentences.

Meetings began with everyone **reading the memo silently**, then discussing it. Writing a real narrative forced the author to think the idea through, and reading together meant everyone started from the same facts. For new products, Amazon teams were often asked to write a **mock press release** first, describing the finished product from the customer's point of view, before building anything.

Around the same time, Bezos pushed for small, independent **"two-pizza teams"**: teams small enough to be fed by two pizzas. The idea was to cut down on coordination and let small groups **own** a problem and move fast, instead of needing constant meetings across the company.

## The Lesson
**Clear writing forces clear thinking.** Small teams with clear ownership move faster than big teams that need to coordinate everything.

## When to Use This
- Meetings drift and decisions don't stick
- You need to pitch an idea to skeptical leaders
- Your team is too large and spends more time syncing than building
- You're starting a new product and want to test whether it's really for customers

## Try This
- Before your next pitch, write a one-to-two-page narrative and send it ahead (or read it together at the start).
- Write the press release for your project as if it just launched. If it's not exciting, rethink the project.
